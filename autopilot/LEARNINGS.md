# What we've learned about Pace Notes visitors

Each entry is dated, says what kind of evidence it rests on, and gives a sample size. The autopilot reads this every run and adds to it after every evaluation. Delete nothing: when a learning turns out wrong, strike it through and add the correction under it.

## Starting point (2026-10-14 diagnosis; 30 days of PostHog data, about 510 visits)
- **Who arrives and how.** Visits come 78% from phones, 22% from desktop and very few from tablets. 83% land on the home page. Sources: about 63% direct and 33% social, nearly all of the social from Instagram (`l.instagram.com`); search brings almost nothing yet. Traffic is spiky: Instagram posts (9/17–18 and 10/09) dominate. Compare windows by source before trusting a before/after change.
- **The redesign widened use but buried the personal path.** Measured on visits, old design (9/17–10/7, 382) vs new design (10/8 onward, 128):
  - Applying analysis filters fell from 31.9% to 12.5%.
  - Home landings going straight to an analysis fell from 45% to 9%.
  - Tools (23%) and stories (20%) went from nothing to real use.
  - Page views per visit rose from 2.5 to 3.5.

  The old home's main button went straight to `/analyses/pacing-pattern`. The likely lesson: a direct "how do runners like me pace?" entry on the first phone screen matters a lot. ~~`home-hero-v1` tests exactly this.~~

  Correction (2026-10-30): `home-hero-v1` was dropped before merge on 2026-10-15 and never ran. `lib/experiments.ts` holds `EXPERIMENTS = []`, so no experiment has run yet (`docs/ANALYTICS.md`, experiments).
- **People who stay on the new home page read it.** The median time before a second page rose from 6.5 s to 46.5 s, and visits whose second page was `/stories` averaged 9.4 page views. The story isn't failing; the first screen is.
- **Runner search** fails to find a name 23.6% of the time (29 of 123), and before the setup a failed search led nowhere.
- **Quick exits were invisible.** About 52% of visits had one page and no action. Replays start after a delay, so they never show quick exits. Since the setup, `$pageleave` duration and `section_viewed` give the missing picture; use them before guessing.
- **Older iPhones** (iOS < 16.4) crashed on 79 of 115 pages until the setup's fix, and crashed pages sent no analytics at all. So historical device data under-counts old iOS. Watch `site_error` by `$os_version` after the fix.
- **Statistics at this size.** About 18 visits a day means two-week before/after windows can only detect jumps of about 12 points or more. An A/B test needs about 150–400 exposed visits per arm. Prefer bold changes, run few experiments, and write "inconclusive" without embarrassment.

- 2026-11-09 (PostHog project 614669, whole UTC days 2026-10-21..2026-11-08, the present home page; n = 411 visits; measured for the `@v2` definitions and re-run independently the same day): the Starting point's 78% phones and 83% home landings no longer hold. Desktop was 210 visits (51.1%) and phones 201 (48.9%), with no tablets; 259 (63.0%) landed on the home page. Desktop visits rarely got an answer: 20 of 210 (9.5%) against 65 of 201 (32.3%) on phones, and 179 desktop visits (85.2%) were one page with no answer signal against 113 (56.2%). 29 of the desktop visits were direct, one-page landings on `/finish-times` (15) and `/runners` (14) on the same six days, so they may share one source: read desktop figures with and without them. Most visits had no referrer; about 15 came from Instagram and about 8 from a search engine (2%). The error guardrail was already past its 1% threshold: 11 of 411 visits (2.68%) had a `site_error`, and 12 of 582 (2.06%) over the 28 days to 2026-11-08; 6 of the 17 `site_error` events from 2026-10-12 to 2026-11-09 were `chunk_load` on Mobile Safari. About 21 visits a day (582 in 28 days), so the Starting point's detection limits still hold.
- 2026-11-12 (PostHog project 614669, whole UTC days 2026-10-21..2026-11-11, the `@v2` scorecard; n = 447 visits; aggregate queries only, no recording opened): about 3 in 10 counted visits look automated, so the 2026-11-09 entry's desktop figures describe mostly scripts, not readers. 137 visits (30.6% of all, 59.8% of the 229 desktop visits) were desktop visits whose first page view had a viewport exactly the size of the screen, which an ordinary browser window does not have and a script-driven browser usually does (a full-screen window would too: a marker, not proof). None of the 137 opened a second page or got an answer; 66 landed on the home page and none continued; 11 sent page feedback; they came on 12 of the 22 days (48 of the 91 visits on 2026-10-27, 18 of 25 on 2026-11-07). The other desktop visits: 22 of 92 answered (23.9%), 57 one page with no answer signal (62.0%), 26 of 72 home landings continued (36.1%). Phones: 68 of 218 answered (31.2%), 121 one page with no answer signal (55.5%), 81 of 142 home landings continued (57.0%). All visits: 90 of 447 answered (20.1%); without the 137, 90 of 310 (29.0%). So among visits that look like readers the desktop gap is in home continuation (36.1% against 57.0%; 72 and 142 landings, too few to be sure), not a near-total failure. 13 of the 14 "No" page-feedback answers on `/runners` came from desktop Chrome on seven days: read them as scripted until shown otherwise, not as a bug report. Errors stay past the guardrail either way: 13 of 447 visits (2.9%), or 10 of 310 (3.2%); `chunk_load` on Mobile Safari is still the largest kind (7 of 17 events), and `site_error` carries only its category, so a cause has to be reproduced, not read from the data. Who runs the automated visits is not known; the repository's smoke test blocks PostHog and is not the source. Issue #97 asks the owner for a scorecard that leaves them out. Until then, read every rate with and without them, and expect any before/after window to move with their schedule.
