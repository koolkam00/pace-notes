## Why it was not adopted: the identity fields are gone

`features.parquet` has 16 columns: `record_id`, city, year, `finish_s`, nine section paces, the two halves and their ratio. In 1652 it had 140. The release README says the cross-race identity and history features "are not included". What is missing:

- **The identity fields:** `runner_id`, `is_ambiguous`, `yob`, `n_races`, `runner_name`, `race`, normalised `sex`, the section times `seg_05`…`seg_42` and `finish_time`.
- **The history fields** built on them.

The site never links people by name itself; it relies on these producer fields:

- **/runners profiles.** `analysis/build_runner_lookup.py` groups a runner's races into one candidate profile by `runner_id`. Without it, every race is its own result.
- **Cross-year analyses.** `analysis/build_extended.py` (`prepare_history`) links eligible finishes through `runner_id`, using the section times to confirm each match. It feeds the earlier-year benchmarks (592,241 in 1652) and the consecutive-year pairs (616,749). Those in turn feed the improvement analysis, the fast-start earlier-best mode and parts of the personalized engine.

Single-race analyses use only `race_records`, which is complete: the stories, tools, course pages, weather and slowdown rates. The proposed workaround was declined by the owner: carry the 1652 identities over to the 4,914,561 identical records and leave the new ones unlinked. See [the producer request](#producer-request) below.

"Partial" alone is not read as incomplete. Many partial editions are within 1–2%, and the producer notes that several official counts (Frankfurt, Vienna, Helsinki) may include other categories.

## Edition review (a proposal for the republished release)

[screen.py](evidence/2026-10-21/release-0829/screen.py) reimplements the 1652 review's three fixed screens, and on the 1652 FULL it reproduces every screen column of the [1652 screen](evidence/2026-10-16/refresh-1652/edition-screen.csv). The screens are: (a) under 5% of finishers usable; (b) at least 75% of the fastest finish quarter but under 50% of the slowest usable; (c) at least 25% of complete grids with a mixed timing basis. On 0829 ([edition-screen.csv](evidence/2026-10-21/release-0829/edition-screen.csv)) they flag exactly the editions 1652 excluded. The next editions below the thresholds are again Washington 2017 (91.4/55.6) and Warsaw 2025 (12.4%). Decisions and measurements are in [edition-policy-review.json](evidence/2026-10-21/release-0829/edition-policy-review.json). In brief:

- **Carried from 1652:**
  - the four producer force-dropped grids;
  - New York 2008, which still lacks an official finisher count;
  - Valencia 2017 and 2018, Tokyo 2016–2018 and 2026 (now also producer holds), Amsterdam 2023, Melbourne 2015, Boston 2013 and Paris 2014–2018;
  - the six grids with mixed timing bases or late mats.
- **Released:**
  - **Chicago 2015:** the load is finished, 0.7% above the official count, and both sexes run to their slowest finishers.
  - **Frankfurt 2019:** held only because the producer's feature engine dropped it. That engine is gone, and the edition is listed complete.
  - **Amsterdam 2025:** the producer's hold is lifted, with 3 of 23,328 missing.
  - **Oslo 2026:** the shortfall is official finishers without a 5 km split, who could never pass the timing checks.
- **New exclusions:**
  - **London 2021–2024:** producer HOLD, 12–38% short, and women make up 31–39% of finishers against 42–43% in 2019, 2025 and 2026.
  - **London 2015–2018:** producer HOLD; no finish passes the timing checks anyway.
  - **Stockholm 2025:** producer HOLD, 9.6% short.
  - **Vienna 2022–2024:** producer HOLD.
  - **Madrid 2023 and Auckland 2017:** misplaced mats.
  - **Chicago 2005:** 15 km is missing for 58.7% of finishers, mostly faster ones, so complete timings survive for 16% of the fastest quarter and 52% of the slowest. This is the mirror image of screen (b); no other edition with usable finishes falls under 50% for its fastest quarter.
  - **Chicago 2007:** the heat-shortened race, whose course closed after about 3.5 hours. Recorded finishes stop at 7:13 (other years run to 8.5–10 hours) and women are 39% of finishers (43–46% in other years). This is a cut-off finisher set, as with Boston 2013.
- **Owner decision on London (October 21, 2026),** verbatim: "Keep London 2025–26". London 2025 and 2026 stay in despite the producer's hold (2.8% and 0.5% short, normal share of women), and London 2021–2024 leave.
- **Kept, with a note:**
  - Chicago 2009: 15 km is missing for 12.7%, with 97.1/79.1 survival by quarter.
  - Frankfurt 2022–2025, Hamburg 2022 and 2025, Stockholm 2011 and New York 2014: partial, but their usable finishes pass every screen.
  - Valencia 2025.
