# Half marathons

The half-marathon pages of Pace Notes (`/half`, the story `/stories/half-to-full`, the predictor's half panel, the goal pages' "The half marathon before", the half pace chart's panel and the "From the half" share cards) read the aggregates in `public/data/half/`. Those are built from the owner's **public** half-marathon release `half-dataset-20261030-1904` (CC BY 4.0, downloadable without an account): its snapshot of the half results and the producer's **person IDs v4**, which link half and marathon records. Verified status is the stamp in the snapshot's own edition metadata, cross-checked against the producer's earlier corrected edition metadata, and the marathon side is the adopted marathon release, read through its public runner shards. This document is the contract: what is pinned, which rules decide what counts, what is published and how it is checked. The operating steps are in [operations](OPERATIONS.md#half-refresh); the module list and commands in [analysis/README.md](../analysis/README.md#half-marathons-public-source-pages-show-aggregates); the page and tool contracts in [the website architecture](WEBSITE_ARCHITECTURE.md), [tools](TOOLS.md) and [stories](STORIES.md). The contract as it stood for the first pin (the 1133 snapshot, runner links v1 and the 2157 overlay, October 29–31, 2026) is in [history](history/half-marathon-2026-10-29-to-31.md).

## Set B: half and marathon records linked by name

Every linked figure uses set B. Its rule is runner links v1's, translated like for like to the v4 person IDs (the lead's rulings L, October 31, 2026, made before any v4 figure was built; review R1 and R4):

1. **The person** has a v4 `person_id` (it must match `^p4_[0-9a-f]{16}$`, asserted in the extract and the build, so an identifier that carried a name would fail), a tier of `high_elite`, `high` or `medium` (the single one of `high_elite`, `high`, `medium` and `low` the person's rows carry: "medium or better"), a sex in `persons.parquet` of M or F that every row with a sex agrees with, and **`ns_keys` of at most 3**.
2. **`ns_keys`, as runner links v1 defined it:** the number of distinct age keys (`age_key`, a year of birth or an age bin) per normalised name (`name_norm`) and published sex, counted over every v4 row of both distances that has an age key; a person takes the largest over the person's rows whose own confidence is `high_elite`, `high` or `medium` (the strong rows: either distance, whatever the record's status, so a half outside the usable editions or a marathon that is not eligible still counts; reworded October 31, 2026, verify round 1, from "the rows that may pair", which a reader could take as the rows that can form a pair: counted over those, the verify round's independent recount gets 58,498 pairs, one more than the rule as built). A name-and-sex block with no age key counts 0 (3,225 of the extract's 502,294 people; they pass the filter and are left out of the `ns_keys_1` member). The SQL is frozen in the extract (`NS_KEYS_SQL`, sha256 `6a66481c…60a2`); the value is stored per person and per link row, and the Node recount recomputes each person's value from its rows.
3. **Both rows of a pair** carry their own row confidence of `high_elite`, `high` or `medium`. Rows attached without an age or a sex (`low_noage`, `low_noage_series`, `low_nosex`) and `low` rows never pair: the producer attached them by matching finish times across the two distances, so they would select on the outcome.
4. **The half** is usable, dated, not R7 and not date-suspect, and not in one of the policy's link editions (`link_editions`, recounted on every build): a **link-partial** edition, one with usable rows on both sides of the link build's coverage boundary (New York Half 2019: 12,160 of its usable rows covered, 12,482 not; the covered part need not be a random part of the field, since load order may follow finish order), or a **re-timed** edition, one whose finish times were reloaded after the link build (México BJX21K 2026: 4,173 of its rows have other finish text). Halves above record id 7,411,867 have no v4 row and cannot pair.
5. **Conflicts** are checked over every linked row of the person, whatever its confidence, the link editions and the R7 rows included: no second usable half of the person on the same date, and none on the date of one of the person's eligible marathons.
6. **The marathon** is an eligible finish of the adopted release (the public runner shards), joined on the canonical record id.
7. **Pairing is unchanged:** for each eligible marathon, the nearest earlier half 21–112 days before it, ties to the lower half record id; one pair per person and marathon.

The producer applies its own unions (middle-name and nationality unions, its rule R22), so there are no candidate merges to leave out; `checks.no_union_people` reruns set B without the extract's 159 union people. The rule in words is the manifest's `link_rules` and `/methodology#half-linking`.

**Counts** (frozen in `half_pairs.EXPECTED`, keyed by `person-ids-v4-20261030-1150`): **58,497 pairs, 50,526 people, 181 marathon editions in 26 cities, 2,570 half editions**; 17,822 recorded women's and 40,675 recorded men's pairs; **4,598 pairs (7.9%) have a verified half**. Runner links v1 gave 23,929 pairs (history). Pairs are pairs of records, never verified people: the site says "records linked by name" on the first linked mention of a page.

**Two signals, counted and not excluded** (review R6; frozen in `half_pairs.EXPECTED` and the manifest's `set_b`, and stated on `/methodology`): **339 halves serve two or more pairs**, and **77 pairs belong to a person with another eligible marathon within 13 days** (any linked row, either side). On runner links v1 the counts were 19 halves (38 pairs) and 9 pairs, so both rose far faster than the pairs (2.4 times). The default adds no exclusion rule: a conflict rule written after the data was seen is a method change, and the `one_per_person` member already tests reuse. A normalised follow-up is in [known issues](KNOWN_ISSUES.md).

**The extract** (`links-v4.sqlite`, record-level, never in the repository) holds `people` (502,294 people: every v4 person with a strong tier, sex M or F and at least one strong half row and one strong marathon row: `person_id`, `sex`, `tier`, `ns_keys`, `key_type`, `in_union`, `k`, `k_block`), `links` (2,448,564 rows, 1,260,231 half and 1,188,333 marathon: every linked row of those people, whatever its confidence, with `confidence`, `finish_s`, `sex`, `city`, `year` and `ns_keys`, so the conflicts and the joins can be recounted) and `build` (the keys, the coverage, every source's sha256, the runner manifest's, the script's and both SQL texts', the producer's `build_info` string and the counts).

**Source and join checks** (each fails loudly with frozen counts in `EXPECTED['person-ids-v4-20261030-1150']` of `half_links_extract.py`; review R3 and R16):

- the release: rows, linked rows, persons, half rows and their largest id, marathon rows and every confidence count, all equal to `checks.txt`; no repeated `(source_db, record_id)`, no person with two sexes or two tiers, no malformed id or age key; `persons.parquet` agrees with the result rows (`n_results`, `n_halves`, `n_marathons`, sex);
- half rows: every snapshot id up to the boundary has a v4 row and no id above it does; city, year and race equal on every row; age equal on every covered row (0 exceptions); finish text equal except on the re-timed edition, and within 0.5 s wherever the builders parse it (6 negative texts in Rafaela 2020 and 2021 and Santander 2023, which the builders never parse, are frozen); published sex equal except 137 rows where the snapshot says X and v4 has none; the v4 name shares at least one token with the snapshot's name on every row but one (Luebeck 2025, frozen);
- marathon rows: every one of the 4,108,749 eligible marathons in the runner shards has a v4 row with the same city and year, its finish within 0.5 s and its recorded sex mapped (0 exceptions).

The Node recount re-verifies every link row (1,260,231 half rows against the snapshot: the id inside the boundary, city, year, sex and the finish within 0.5 s, except 483 rows of the re-timed edition; 964,513 eligible-marathon rows against the shards: city, year, finish and sex; 0 exceptions) and recomputes each person's `ns_keys`; since verify round 1 (October 31, 2026) it also checks each person's sex, which every pair takes from `persons.parquet`, against the published sex of each of its link rows (review R16; 0 exceptions; until then only the extract compared them, and no test covered that clause, which now has one). Names and ages are not in the extract, so those two joins run only where it is cut; the extract's pinned digest binds them, and the CI `source` job re-cuts it from the public parquet files.

**`k`, evidence only.** `k` is the number of aged v4 persons in a sorted-name-token and published-sex block (a person is aged when one of its rows was not attached without an age), rebuilt as the producer's `code/final3.sql` builds it but deterministic: a person's block uses the smallest sorted name over its rows, where `final3.sql` takes any. It is not the filter and not a battery member, and no page shows it. The extract stores it (`K_SQL`, sha256 `b0b58ef6…cdfc`) with its block; both recounts check one `k` per block and no more extract people in a block than `k` (491,385 blocks), and that every `high` person is alone in its block, as the producer's tiers imply (0 exceptions). Its effect as a filter is in the [evidence](#evidence-measured-october-31-2026).

**What the Node recount cannot do.** It cannot rebuild `ns_keys` or `k` from the release itself: it has no parquet reader, and the extract holds no names. Both are bound by the pinned digest, the CI re-cut and the checks above. The producer is asked to export its block counts ([below](#for-the-producer)).

**Time-screened links** (review R5). The producer's linking accepted or rejected some merges on half-to-marathon time fits: its rule R16 blocks a merge whose cross-distance finish ratio falls outside a range (1.55–3.4 for rare names and 1.75–3.3 for common ones, wider where same-distance evidence exists), aged rows also merge through a tight test that needs a ratio of 1.95–2.35 (R11, R12), R20 needs 1.8–2.6 and R13 breaks ties on time fit (read in the producer's `code/cluster5.py`, specification review of October 31, 2026). So medium and high persons are partly selected on the very ratio the story's multiple measures, and the tails of the multiple partly reflect the link build. A count of pairs with an implausible ratio therefore cannot estimate false links (it is circular): `/methodology` says this in words, and no page gives a ratio-based false-link figure. The producer's hand-review rates are cited as the producer's claim (`links.producer_review`: in the last two rounds, none of 150 merged persons in each of the rare-name and common-name cells joined two people, an upper 95% bound of about 2% each, and 2.7% and 2.0% of rare-name links were missed). The producer is asked for a per-row flag of merges that used a time test.

## How it is verified

Three layers:

1. **Layer 1, in CI: `scripts/verify-half.cjs`** (in `npm run verify:data` and the **Half marathon** workflow's `check` job, about 40 s). From the repository alone it checks provenance (the manifest against the schema 3 pin: `key`, `tag`, the assets, the coverage, `stamps.reference`, `link_rules` and `private_tag` only where the pin sets it, with `repository` and `license`; the producer-review block and the DOI forms; the adopted release, the runner manifest, the weather evidence, the cited marathon files, every builder script and every file's bytes and sha); the policy's reviewed counts and frozen lists, `stamp_check` and set B's reviewed counts, the R6 signals included, and that the counts add up (R1–R6, R7, and the editions' used, E0, E1 and E2–E8 finishes summing to the record-usable finishes after R7); floors, identifier keys, allow-listed strings, release labels and the banned words on every file string, nugget, dek, tool sentence and card word; exact recounts of every half-only value from the published histogram and bins (shares under every mark, quantiles against their bins, the densest 30 minutes, the women's bands and crossing, the same-city shares; the round marks' fits, whose windows reach below the published range, are bound to the receipt instead) and of the marathon side of every same-city pair from the public shards; the internal consistency of the linked aggregates (floors, monotone quantiles, mark shares within the bounds their quantile levels allow, contiguous ranges, edge slices, sexes summing to all, the goal rows recomputed from the windows with clean crossings, the battery against the checks and its reruns against their own counts and the guard's clauses, the tool file equal to `linked.json`, the cards equal to their sources); and that a card exists as a PNG exactly when its guard holds. It cannot recount a linked value: those come from the source rows.
2. **Layer 2, locally: `scripts/verify-half-source.cjs`** (`npm run verify:half-source`; about 2 minutes and 1.8 GB resident on this build, for 7.67 million records and 2.45 million link rows; about 90 s and 1 GB on the 1133 snapshot). An independent Node recount: it opens the snapshot and the extract read-only with `node:sqlite` and reads the stamp reference and the release's `csv-edition_meta.csv.zst` with `zstd`, checks all four against the pin, reapplies R1–R7 with its own parser, sub-race pattern and name key, recomputes the E3–E8 screens, the virtual events, the partial loads, the link editions and the stamp check and compares them with the frozen lists, checks the policy JSON's rule text against its own constants, re-verifies the extract's people and link rows ([above](#set-b-half-and-marathon-records-linked-by-name)), rebuilds set B and every published file and compares them value for value. It writes the receipt [`docs/evidence/half/source-recount.json`](evidence/half/source-recount.json): date, status, values checked, mismatches, per-file counts, the extract's people, blocks and join counts, and the shas it bound (manifest, every recount script, half snapshot, links extract, stamp reference, the release's edition metadata, runner manifest, policy, weather evidence). No row data. Layer 1 fails unless the receipt says pass with no mismatches and binds the current manifest and recount scripts, so **a rebuild or a changed recount without a fresh local recount fails CI**. `--policy-only` stops after the policy and the stamp check and writes no receipt.
3. **Layer 3, in CI: the workflow's `source` job** (anonymous). On `workflow_dispatch`, and on pushes to `main` and pull requests whose diff touches the pin, the policy JSON, `build_half.py`, an `analysis/half_*.py` module, one of the five non-half modules the builders import (`build_fast_start.py`, `insights_data.py`, `insights_stats.py`, `insights_round.py`, `charts_common.py`; added October 31, 2026, review R13), the anonymous downloader `analysis/download_half.py`, `analysis/requirements.txt`, the recount scripts or the workflow itself, it fails if a token variable or a git auth header is set, downloads the pinned public assets with `download_half.py` (about 1.3 GB: the snapshot part and the 0.81 GB of v4 parquet files, on the runner's second disk with DuckDB's spill), reassembles the snapshot, refuses a non-empty `-wal`, cuts the extract (its digest must equal the pin, every join check passing), rebuilds into the runner's temp folder, requires every published file to be byte-identical and runs the source recount with `--no-write`. ## When to rebuild

Rebuild (and rerun the source recount) after:

- a new half snapshot, person-ID build or stamp reference (new pin, policy review, extract, rebuild, receipt);
- a change to `analysis/release.json` or the runner manifest (`lib/half-server.ts` throws at build until the half data is rebuilt; a new marathon release also needs a new person-ID build from the producer, because v4 keys marathon rows by the marathon release's canonical record ids);
- a change to `analysis/weather-release.json` or the weather evidence (marathon dates);
- a change to `insights/finish-times.json` or `charts/womens-share-by-finish-time.json` (cited marathon values);
- any change to `analysis/half_*.py`, `analysis/build_half.py` or the policy (the manifest binds every builder script);
- any change to `scripts/verify-half-source.cjs` or `scripts/half/{derive,policy,marathon,families}.cjs` (the receipt binds them; a recount is enough, no rebuild).

After a rebuild, read every half page again: a moved number can turn a guard (the reading falls back), a card (withdrawn) or a published range (narrowed). The steps are in [operations](OPERATIONS.md#half-refresh).

## Lessons from the half integration

- **A correction is an input.** The producer's corrected metadata is pinned by sha256 like the snapshot, its effect is a frozen, reviewed list, and every way the file and the list could disagree fails the build. When a later snapshot carries the correction natively, the file stays pinned as a cross-check, and a withdrawn stamp that comes back fails the build. A published dataset version that a later file corrects is named as superseded on the site, not silently replaced.
- **Old names are identities, not access.** Moving the source from a private to a public release changed `tag` (where to download) and kept the old identity (what the policy and counts are keyed by). When a snapshot had no private twin, the pin named the identity `key` and kept `private_tag` only as provenance.
- **A person-ID build has a coverage boundary: pin it, and exclude the editions that straddle it.** The v4 build covers the half rows up to one id; an edition loaded partly before and partly after supplies no linked half, because its covered part need not be a random part of the field, and an edition reloaded after the build is out too. The producer's own coverage string understated the boundary, so the pin records what was measured.
- **A rebuilt producer-internal field must be deterministic and frozen.** `k` came from `final3.sql`'s `any_value`; rebuilt with the smallest name, frozen as SQL with its sha, it reproduces, and a sanity check against the producer's tiers binds it.
- **Translate a rule like for like; a renamed member can loosen a guard.** The specification renamed the `ns_keys = 1` member to `k = 1`, which held 84% of set B and could no longer disagree with the pool; the review measured it and the member kept its v1 meaning (review R1, October 31, 2026). Pre-register the checks a guard needs before the data exists, then keep them: the reruns decided by the decision of October 30 are what the H1 fails on.
- **When a number moves, re-judge the guard, then let the page fall back.** The correction made four readings and two cards fail their own rules; each now shows its neutral fallback or is withdrawn, and the reasons are recorded above, instead of thresholds being moved to keep the headlines.
