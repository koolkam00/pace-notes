# Half marathons

The half-marathon pages of Pace Notes (`/half`, the story `/stories/half-to-full`, the predictor's half panel, the goal pages' "The half marathon before", the half pace chart's panel and the "From the half" share cards) read the aggregates in `public/data/half/`. Those are built from the owner's **public** half-marathon snapshot and link build (CC BY 4.0, downloadable without an account), with verified status taken from the producer's **corrected** edition metadata, joined to the adopted marathon release through its public runner shards. This document is the contract: what is pinned, which rules decide what counts, what is published and how it is checked. The operating steps are in [operations](OPERATIONS.md#half-refresh); the module list and commands in [analysis/README.md](../analysis/README.md#half-marathons-public-source-pages-show-aggregates); the page and tool contracts in [the website architecture](WEBSITE_ARCHITECTURE.md), [tools](TOOLS.md) and [stories](STORIES.md).

### Verified: stamped in both (the stamp correction, October 30, 2026)

The producer stamps an edition when its finisher count is within 0.5% of an independent published total and its year and both winners are confirmed. After the first public release, the producer found that some stamps rested on a count that was not independent (an aggregator, a timing platform's own list or total, or a republished result) and published corrected edition metadata (`half-dataset-20261029-2157`); figshare version 1 of the dataset carries the earlier metadata, which overstated the verified editions.

**Rule:** an edition is verified only when it is stamped in this snapshot **and** in the corrected metadata. An edition stamped here but not there is unverified: it keeps its records, passes through the edition rules like any internal-only edition and can still supply linked halves. An edition stamped only in the corrected metadata is not verified (no usable edition of this snapshot is).

- **Withdrawn:** 72 usable editions of this snapshot, 869,551 finishes, among them Paris 2017, 2019 and 2022 to 2026, Valencia 2016 to 2018 and 2021 to 2026, Berlin 2026 and New York 2025. The reviewed list (edition id, label, year, corrected status and the producer's reason, trimmed by a frozen rule) is `WITHDRAWN_STAMPS_20261029_2157` in `analysis/half_policy_lists.py` and in the policy JSON.
- **Weaker source, still verified:** six stamps whose count comes from an organiser or a local paper rather than a federation (Kraków 2022 and 2025, the Cracovia half under two producer labels, "Kraków: Cracovia Półmaraton Królewski" and "Kraków"; Lisbon 2024; Piła 2026; Szczecin 2023 and 2026), named in the corrected release's README; `WEAKER_SOURCE_20261029_2157`. `/methodology` names each by its city, so one race never reads as two.
- **Fails loudly** (`build_half.py` and the Node recount alike): the corrected file missing or with another sha256; an edition listed twice; the frozen withdrawn list differing from what the file gives; a withdrawn edition that is not a stamped edition of that name here; an edition stamped in the corrected metadata but unverified in the build, or verified in the build but not stamped there; a weaker-source stamp that is not a verified usable edition.
- **Counts:** **verified 753,476 finishes in 238 editions; `use_sex`** (edition at least 95% published sex and each sex at least 3%; record sex M or F, never inferred) **734,301 verified finishes in 231 editions.** Before the correction: 1,623,027 finishes in 310 editions, and 1,558,344 in 299 (the evidence of October 29–30 in Git history at `9f7cf7de` and earlier).

## Set B: half and marathon records linked by name

Every linked figure uses set B:

1. people from `links-slim.sqlite` with confidence high or medium and `ns_keys ≤ 3`; candidate merges not applied;
2. a half that is usable, dated, not R7, not date-suspect, with no second half of the person on the same date and none on the date of one of the person's marathon records;
3. a marathon that is an eligible finish of the adopted release (the public runner shards), joined on the canonical record id;
4. for each eligible marathon, the nearest earlier half 21–112 days before it; one pair per person and marathon.

**23,929 pairs, 20,234 people, 107 marathon editions in 21 cities, 1,045 half editions**; 5,947 recorded women's and 17,982 recorded men's pairs. The stamp correction leaves set B unchanged (verified status does not enter its rules) but moves its verified share: **2,024 pairs (8.5%) have a verified half**, against 14,321 (59.8%) before; Valencia's halves, the largest pairing in the data, are all unverified now. Pairs are pairs of records, never verified people: the site says "records linked by name" on the first linked mention of a page.

## How it is verified

Three layers:

1. **Layer 1, in CI: `scripts/verify-half.cjs`** (in `npm run verify:data` and the **Half marathon** workflow's `check` job, about 40 s). From the repository alone it checks provenance (the manifest against the pin, including `private_tag`, `repository`, `license` and `metadata_correction`; the adopted release, the runner manifest, the weather evidence, the cited marathon files, every builder script and every file's bytes and sha); floors, identifier keys, allow-listed strings, release labels and the banned words on every file string, nugget, dek, tool sentence and card word; exact recounts of every half-only value from the published histogram and bins (shares under every mark, quantiles against their bins, the densest 30 minutes, the women's bands and crossing, the same-city shares; the round marks' fits, whose windows reach below the published range, are bound to the receipt instead) and of the marathon side of every same-city pair from the public shards; the internal consistency of the linked aggregates (floors, monotone quantiles, mark shares within the bounds their quantile levels allow, contiguous ranges, edge slices, sexes summing to all, the goal rows recomputed from the windows with clean crossings, the battery against the checks, the tool file equal to `linked.json`, the cards equal to their sources); and that a card exists as a PNG exactly when its guard holds. It cannot recount a linked value: those come from the source rows.
2. **Layer 2, locally: `scripts/verify-half-source.cjs`** (`npm run verify:half-source`, about 90 s and 1 GB). An independent Node recount: it opens both SQLite files read-only with `node:sqlite` and reads the corrected metadata with `zstd`, checks all three against the pin, reapplies R1–R7 with its own parser, sub-race pattern and name key, recomputes the E3–E8 screens and the stamp correction and compares them with the frozen lists, rebuilds set B and every published file and compares them value for value. It writes the receipt [`docs/evidence/half/source-recount.json`](evidence/half/source-recount.json): date, status, values checked, mismatches, per-file counts and the shas it bound (manifest, every recount script, half snapshot, links extract, corrected metadata, runner manifest, policy, weather evidence). No row data. Layer 1 fails unless the receipt says pass with no mismatches and binds the current manifest and recount scripts, so **a rebuild or a changed recount without a fresh local recount fails CI**.
3. **Layer 3, in CI: the workflow's `source` job** (anonymous). On `workflow_dispatch`, and on pushes to `main` and pull requests whose diff touches the pin, the policy JSON, `build_half.py`, an `analysis/half_*.py` module, the anonymous downloader `analysis/download_half.py`, `analysis/requirements.txt`, the recount scripts or the workflow itself, it downloads the pinned public assets with `download_half.py` (the 5.3 GB links SQLite on the runner's second disk), rebuilds into the runner's temp folder, requires every published file to be byte-identical and runs the source recount with `--no-write`. Other changes print "skipped: this change touches no half pin, policy, builder, downloader or recount file" (the downloader and the requirements joined the scope on October 30, 2026: a change to the downloader alone had never run the anonymous download). Its steps were run on this machine on October 30, 2026 ([evidence](#evidence-measured-october-30-2026)).

## When to rebuild

Rebuild (and rerun the source recount) after:

- a new half snapshot, link build or corrected metadata (new pin, policy review, rebuild, receipt);
- a change to `analysis/release.json` or the runner manifest (`lib/half-server.ts` throws at build until the half data is rebuilt; a new marathon release also needs a new link build from the producer, because link record ids follow the marathon release's canonical ids);
- a change to `analysis/weather-release.json` or the weather evidence (marathon dates);
- a change to `insights/finish-times.json` or `charts/womens-share-by-finish-time.json` (cited marathon values);
- any change to `analysis/half_*.py`, `analysis/build_half.py` or the policy (the manifest binds every builder script);
- any change to `scripts/verify-half-source.cjs` or `scripts/half/{derive,policy,marathon,families}.cjs` (the receipt binds them; a recount is enough, no rebuild).

After a rebuild, read every half page again: a moved number can turn a guard (the reading falls back), a card (withdrawn) or a published range (narrowed). The steps are in [operations](OPERATIONS.md#half-refresh).

## Lessons from the half integration

- **A correction is an input.** The producer's corrected metadata is pinned by sha256 like the snapshot, its effect is a frozen, reviewed list, and every way the file and the list could disagree fails the build. A published dataset version that a later file corrects is named as superseded on the site, not silently replaced.
- **Old names are identities, not access.** Moving the source from a private to a public release changed `tag` (where to download) and kept `private_tag` (what the policy and counts are keyed by), so no reviewed count or list had to be re-keyed.
- **When a number moves, re-judge the guard, then let the page fall back.** The correction made four readings and two cards fail their own rules; each now shows its neutral fallback or is withdrawn, and the reasons are recorded above, instead of thresholds being moved to keep the headlines.
- **Narrow or withhold, don't lower.** Three floors failed after the correction (a minute of 82, a tail of 44, 15 band editions under the builder's 20). The histogram and the sex bins narrowed. The band floor was first lowered to 3 with a leave-one-out check added, so the columns stayed; the review of October 30, 2026 restored the 20 in the builder, the Node recount and `verify-half`, and the band medians are now left out (`test_half.py` holds the 20). A compensating check never replaces a floor.
- **A fallback covers every slot.** When `half.extra`'s guard failed, its H1 fell back but its figure fell back to "+21 min", the very minutes the guard had rejected, and the chapter 11 row, the next cards and the `/half` night card repeated it; `half.cities` and `half.range` kept deks that stated what their headings withdrew; a table still marked its reading row. Each place a reading can appear now follows a guard: a figure has its own (or is a neutral count), a dek is null or functional under a plain heading, and `scripts/nuggets/half.cjs` recounts the deks against their guards. The second review round (October 31, 2026) found two more: a sentence under the story's scrubbed chart that restated the withdrawn quantities at each half, and hub lines that promised "one finding each" above a row that now showed a count. Search for the quantity, not only the sentence.
- **Write rules so a stranger can recount them.** The E8 screen was described without saying that held editions sit in its reference set; a literal recount flagged one more edition. The policy JSON now says it in words (`E8.reference`).
- **A merge changes dates too.** Pages that gain a half row or line (`/`, `/research`, `/about`, who holds pace, the predictor, the goal pages, the half pace chart, `/finish-times`) take the merge day in their sitemap `lastmod` (`CONTENT_DATES`, `HALF_TEXT_WRITTEN`), not only the new pages.
- **Prove the CI job on the machine first.** The source job's exact steps (anonymous download, rebuild, byte comparison, recount) were run locally before the workflow relied on them. The first run failed: the public `runner_links.sqlite` declares no type for several columns, which DuckDB reads as BLOB, and the extract had only ever been cut from the parquet form. Test fixtures now use the release's own schema. An anonymous check is a ranged GET on the github.com URL, dated.
