## Top 100 charts: a section of its own (October 23, 2026)

The owner asked on October 23, 2026 for "a new section called top 100 charts": a hundred analyses of the data, each with one key takeaway, drawn to expert visualisation standards, for a series on Instagram, and kept apart so it does not crowd the core site.

**Repository state:** merged to `main` as `24caac1` (pull request #81, October 23, 2026). **Deployed state:** live: production `version.json` named `24caac1c8a48` (built 14:39 UTC), `/charts`, `/charts/1` … `/charts/100` and their cards answered 200, `/research` links the section and the sitemap lists all 101 pages; the production smoke test on `24caac1` passed 210 of 210 page checks and 26 of 26 in old Safari, with no third-party requests (October 23, 2026, about 14:45 UTC).

What it is: `/charts` (a numbered list) and `/charts/1` … `/charts/100`, one chart and one takeaway each, every page also drawn as a 1080 × 1350 card (`public/charts/cards/<n>.png`) with a caption in `docs/CHARTS_INSTAGRAM.md`. It is off the chapter chain, the Menu and the footer; its one way in from the core site is a line on `/research`, and its methods are one section of `/methodology` (`#top-charts`). Every number comes from the adopted release (`private-export-20261021-1050`) through `analysis/build_charts.py` and is recounted independently by `scripts/verify-charts.cjs`; the cards carry stamps that `build-chart-cards.cjs --check` recomputes. Both run in `npm run verify:data`, and the **Top 100 charts** workflow rebuilds every chart in CI and requires the committed bytes. The contract, the checks, the refresh steps and the dated evidence are in [Top 100 charts](TOP_100_CHARTS.md); the refresh is also under [Operations](OPERATIONS.md#top-100-charts-refresh).

How the hundred were chosen and checked (summary; detail in [Top 100 charts](TOP_100_CHARTS.md#evidence)): 135 tested candidates, a curated 100 critiqued by three independent reviewers, weak or repeated entries replaced after a second round, then each chart built, reviewed, fixed and polished. A whole-set adversarial review (77 confirmed findings, 6 of them blockers) set one series style and was fixed module by module, each module re-checked by an independent editor; two near-duplicates were cut and replaced by newly tested and judged analyses. A second whole-set review (60 confirmed findings, 3 blockers) was fixed and re-checked the same way; it cut two more weak cards, replaced by the two highest-scored of ten fresh candidates ("Sub-3:30 doubles: the second more often slower", "The 25–30K split ordered the field best"), and sharpened the New York 25–30K card. The series order puts the strongest first, keeps neighbours on different themes and forms, and spaces related cards (New York, field spread, the 3:59 cards) apart.

Still for the owner:
- Read the hundred before posting: the cards publish under the owner's name, and a refresh can change a headline (a chart whose guard no longer holds shows its fallback sentence).
- Bars are the most common form (25 of 100), above the 15 per form the selection aimed for; no two neighbouring cards share a form, but a series that runs daily may want more variety.
- Dumbbells draw their first dot as a quiet ring and their second as an ink dot on every row but the signal one (`components/charts/rows.tsx`), so on No. 42 the comparison dot of the accented row is a ring with an ink label rather than ink itself; a per-row tone would change all eleven dumbbell cards and was left for a later pass.

**Repository state:** on `main` since PR #56 (merge eeaf48e). **Deployed state:** live on splithappens.run, checked in its served HTML on October 15, 2026 (the home page carries the `pace-notes-chunk-reload` head script, and its initial JavaScript has no lookbehind).

What changed:
- **The crash.** Safari before 16.4 (iOS 15, iOS 16.0–16.3) cannot build a regular-expression lookbehind, and the miles conversion in `lib/units.ts` built five of them in code the root layout runs. CI runs both, and `verify:analytics` runs the same source scan. `verify-units` reruns its conversion table with lookbehind refused.
- **Error pages.** `app/error.tsx` (inside the site's header and footer) and `app/global-error.tsx` (when the root layout fails) replace Next.js's blank "Application error" screen. Each offers "Reload the page" and a link home, moves focus to its heading and sets the tab's title. After a failed script load, the page reloads by itself once. A sessionStorage timestamp (`pace-notes-chunk-reload`) stops it doing so again within five minutes. A small script in the root layout's `<head>` makes that reload happen at once when a script or stylesheet named in the page's HTML fails; without it webpack waits two minutes, and a failed React file never reaches an error page. A chart whose chunk fails shows a note in its box instead of the error page replacing the whole page.

## Fast-start analysis

Rank 2, `/analyses/starting-pace`, defaults to [all eligible finishes](../public/data/fast-start/all-finishers.json): under 1652, **3,871,767** performances, including **3,279,526** without a recent prior benchmark (0934: 3,517,336 and 2,961,899). It compares first 5 km pace with the same race's 5–20 km pace using six fixed intensity bands. The primary time outcome is actual elapsed time after 20 km minus the time at that recorded reference pace; the actual median finish is separate. First-5 km and after-20 km mean differences add to the whole-race mean difference because the 5–20 km reference block contributes zero. No earlier history, cross-race identity link, speed filter or imputed data is needed.

The optional [earlier-best comparison](../public/data/fast-start/evidence.json) retains its original output byte for byte. It compares first 10 km pace with the best eligible finish in the two strictly earlier calendar years, covering **592,241 benchmarked finishes**, 582 exact filter combinations and 2,873 groups under 1652 (0934: 555,437, 576 and 2,846). Its history restrictions do not limit the new default mode. The earlier personalized opening/target-threshold path remains in the research guide without recalculation.

Both builders check every compressed runner shard (7,881 under 1652; 7,766 under 0934) and source script hashes and reconcile raw/eligible counts. Both outputs bind the exact runner-manifest hash, so lookup refreshes require rebuilding both. Each displayed group needs 100 finishes. Onset distributions additionally need 100 detected finishes and use only those detected finishes as their denominator; the slowdown rate uses every finish in the selected mode/group. All raw records remain searchable/downloadable under the existing lookup contract, but incomplete or held results do not enter these aggregate outcomes.

The all-finisher comparison finds unusually quick first sections, not proven excessive effort. It can miss a runner who maintains an ambitious opening pace through 20 km before fading. The same-race baseline is measured after the opening and is shared by the opening and slowdown ratios. Neither mode estimates causal time penalties, current fitness, physiological failure or withdrawals. Counts are performances, not unique runners. `comparison=all|history` selects the mode; legacy `prior=` or `previous=` links imply history only when no explicit mode is set. Explicit All clears prior filtering.

Under 1652 the all-finisher calculation publishes **325 filter combinations / 1,646 groups** and passes its Python tests and its full independent source-data verifier. Its All-filters `fast10` group has **195,980 finishes**, **49.6% sustained slowdown**, and median time after 20 km **28:11 above the 5–20 km pace reference**. Its actual median finish is **5:46:19**.
