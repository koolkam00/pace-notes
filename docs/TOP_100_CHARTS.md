# Top 100 charts

A section of its own at `/charts`: a hundred findings from the adopted runner release, one chart and one takeaway each, numbered 1 to 100 and drawn so that each page also works as an Instagram card (1080 × 1350). It stays off the chapter chain: its one way in from the core site is a line on `/research`, and its methods live on `/methodology#top-charts`. Started October 23, 2026 on branch `feat/top-100-charts`; merged as `24caac1` (pull request #81) and live on splithappens.run the same day (production `version.json` named that commit, and the production smoke test passed 210 of 210 page checks).

## What a chart is

Every chart is one data file, one set of words and one drawing:

- **The data file**, `public/data/charts/<slug>.json`, written by `analysis/build_charts.py` from the checksum-verified runner shards (`public/data/runners`) of the release pinned in `analysis/release.json`, plus the pinned weather evidence and the supplied-route geometry where a chart needs them. It holds `values` (every number the takeaway prints and every input of its guard), `plot` (the chart, in the spec of `lib/charts/spec.ts`), `cohort_n`, `editions_n`, `weighting` (`pooled`, `editions-equal` or `pairs`) and `method` (the one line `/methodology` shows). `public/data/charts/manifest.json` binds them to the release tag, the exact runner manifest (hash and timestamps), the catalog, the weather evidence and every calculation script by SHA-256.
- **The words**, `lib/charts/text/<module>.ts`: a title (≤ 47 characters) and a takeaway function of the chart's values that returns the headline (one sentence with exactly one `*accent*`), a neutral fallback and a guard. The page shows the headline only while the guard holds on the published values; otherwise the fallback. `lib/charts/registry.ts` collects them and `chartWords` is the one way pages, cards, nuggets and the verifier read them.
- **The drawing**, `components/charts/`: one server-rendered SVG on a fixed 340-unit-wide canvas (`kit.tsx` for scales, ticks, notes and keys; `rows.tsx`, `xy.tsx` and `grid.tsx` for the 17 forms). The same component draws the page and the card. One `signal` mark is the reading the takeaway is about, `ink` the other thing it names, everything else `quiet`; orange never means slower or worse.

The catalog, `lib/charts/catalog.json`, lists the hundred in series order: `{number, slug, theme, module}`. Themes: finish times, pacing, racing, courses, who runs (`people`), over the years (`trends`). Each module is a pair of files, `analysis/<module>.py` (the builders, registered with `@chart(slug)`) and `scripts/charts/<module>.cjs` (the recounts).

## The rules every chart keeps

The site's contracts (AGENTS.md) apply to every chart: counts are finishes; every group shown holds at least 100 finishes (pairs: 100 pairs); associations only, never causal words; no "fastest", "the wall", "your chance", predictions, halfway, goals, non-finishers, humidity or course-difficulty rankings; exact ages only from the granular-age editions; the gender-gap editions stay out of every composition chart; the start-offset editions (`shape_keep`) stay out of every pacing-shape statistic; positions are clock ranks within a race; records linked by name are candidates, not verified people; weather is race-morning context (warming an association, wind a precise null). Titles and takeaways carry no pace, distance or temperature (the reader's units switch on the page); charts may plot them on an axis. Notes on a chart are readings, never method or coverage text. The sustained-slowdown term links to its definition on `/methodology#sustained-slowdown`.

The series style (set after the whole-set review, October 23, 2026), so a hundred cards posted one a day read as one voice: whole percentage points ("5 points", the `dpts` format "+5 pts"); "6:00+" for the slowest band and en-dash ranges ("5:00–6:00"); time gaps in a takeaway in words or minutes and seconds ("4½ minutes", "2 min 18 s"), never in m:ss, which reads as a clock time; "the typical edition" for a median over editions and "on average" for a mean; "edition" for a race-year and "race" for the event; "big races" only for the eight-race set (Berlin, Chicago, New York, Boston, Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Hamburg, Copenhagen); "under 3:00" in takeaways ("sub-3:00" only as an adjective in a title); "in step at every mat"; "mornings" for start-temperature context; "women's finishes" and "records linked by name" on first mention. One signal reading per card, which may be one group of marks (every section after 20K, every race in a strip), and the accent is always drawn on the chart. The apostrophe is set once for every title, takeaway, label and table cell (`lib/charts/typo.ts`).

## Checks

- `python -m unittest discover -s analysis -p 'test_charts.py'`: the helpers, the catalog against the builder modules, and every builder on a synthetic cohort.
- `node scripts/verify-charts.cjs`: an independent recount. It rebuilds the eligible finishes from the runner shards (`scripts/charts/cohort.cjs`, no Python), checks provenance (release, runner manifest, catalog, weather evidence, every script hash), the inventory, the 100-finish threshold, identifier fields, release labels and the banned words in every string, renders every title and takeaway through `chartWords` and checks their lengths, accents and guards, and then recounts each chart's values and plotted numbers with its own function (integers exactly; other numbers within 0.051 at 50 or more, 1e-6 below). A registered chart without a recount fails. `--only slug,…` and `--input <folder>` are for development.
- `node scripts/build-chart-cards.cjs --check`: every card in `public/charts/cards/<number>.png` carries a stamp of what it was drawn from (its data file, its words, the chart code, styles and fonts); the check recomputes the stamps without a browser and fails on a missing, changed, oversized (> 260 KB) or orphaned card.
- Both run in `npm run verify:data`. The **Top 100 charts** workflow (`.github/workflows/charts.yml`) also rebuilds every chart from the shards in CI, recounts the rebuilt files and requires them to match the committed bytes.

## Refresh after a data release

After a runner, weather or geometry refresh (or any change to `analysis/build_charts.py`, `analysis/charts_common.py` or a `charts_*.py` module, whose hashes the manifest binds):

```bash
python -m unittest discover -s analysis -p 'test_charts.py'
python analysis/build_charts.py --output public/data/charts
node scripts/verify-charts.cjs
node scripts/build-chart-cards.cjs            # redraws the cards whose stamp changed
node scripts/build-chart-cards.cjs --captions # docs/CHARTS_INSTAGRAM.md
npm run verify:data && npm run build
```

A chart whose guard no longer holds shows its fallback sentence; read every headline that changed before publishing, and rewrite a takeaway rather than loosen its guard. The builder needs about 2 GB of memory and about 6 minutes for the hundred from the shards (367 s on October 23, 2026); the recount about 4 minutes (255 s).

## Pages, cards and captions

- `/charts` (`app/charts/page.tsx`): the hundred as one numbered list, each row its title and takeaway, linking to `/charts/<number>`.
- `/charts/<number>` (`app/charts/[n]/page.tsx`, `components/charts/ChartPage.tsx`): the card (kicker "No. N of 100" and theme, the title as H1, the takeaway, the chart, the brand line), "Save the image" (the card PNG), the chart's numbers as a table, the pager and "Was this useful?". Pages whose plot shows a pace or a temperature redraw in the reader's units.
- The cards: `node scripts/build-chart-cards.cjs` draws each page's card in Chromium at 400 × 500 CSS px and a scale of 2.7. The plot takes the height the title and takeaway leave (measured, then laid out to fill it); the build fails when a title runs past two lines, a takeaway past three, or a chart past its box.
- `docs/CHARTS_INSTAGRAM.md`: one caption per chart for the series (title, takeaway, the page address), written by `--captions` from the same words.

Analytics (`lib/analytics-policy.ts`) records `/charts` and `/charts/1` … `/charts/CHART_COUNT`; `verify-analytics` checks `CHART_COUNT` against the catalog, and any other `/charts/` path records as `/charts`. Search: every page is indexable and in the sitemap, its `lastmod` the charts manifest's date ([SEO](SEO.md)).

## From the half cards

Four cards outside the hundred, from the half-marathon pages ([half marathons](HALF_MARATHON.md); owner question Q11 at its default, October 2026). They use the same `ChartCard`, size, frame and stamp scheme, but they are not in `lib/charts/catalog.json`, so `CHART_COUNT` stays 100 and the numbered series is untouched:

| Card | Page | Scope | Draws |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `half-under-2` "Just over half broke 2:00" | `/half` | verified | a waffle of the half finishes under 2:00 (56%); the takeaway adds the recorded women's and men's shares |
| `half-more-women` "More women in the half, city by city" | `/half` | verified | a dumbbell of the same-city, same-year pairs by city, the marathon as the ring and the half as the dot, under a signal row "Typical" at the two medians (the takeaway words them as two: "Their share of finishes was typically 38% in the half and 24% in the marathon.") |
| `half-double` "Double the half, plus 21 minutes" | `/stories/half-to-full` | linked | a waffle of the marathons under double the half (8%, signal), within 20 minutes over it (40%) and the rest |
| `half-same-half` "Same half, fewer sustained slowdowns for women" | `/stories/half-to-full` | linked | a dumbbell of recorded men's (the ring) and women's (the dot) sustained-slowdown shares after halves near 1:30, 1:45, 2:00 and 2:15, the 1:45 band the signal row, so its orange dot is the women's share the takeaway names |

- **Data:** `public/data/half/cards.json` (`analysis/half_cards.py`; recounted by `scripts/verify-half-source.cjs` and checked against its family files by `scripts/verify-half.cjs`).
- **Words:** `lib/charts/text/half.ts`, a registry of its own (`HALF_CARD_TEXT`, read through `halfCardWords`). Each guard is the page's nugget guard plus the card's own checks, so a card falls back exactly when its page does; `verify-half` holds the words to the rules above (titles ≤ 47 characters, one accent, the banned words) and requires "records linked by name" in a linked card's takeaway and nowhere else.
- **Drawing:** `node scripts/build-chart-cards.cjs --set half` (`scripts/half/card-images.cjs`) writes `public/charts/cards/half-<…>.png`. The kicker reads "From the half" and a theme instead of "No. N of 100"; the footer names the page (`splithappens.run/half` or `/stories/half-to-full`), and a linked card prints the chip "Includes unverified half results" over it. The stamp binds the card code, `cards.json`, the family file the card reads, the title and the takeaway; `scripts/half/card-images.cjs` is outside the Top 100's code list, so a change there re-stamps the four half cards only.
- **Checks:** `node scripts/build-chart-cards.cjs --check --set half` (in `npm run verify:data` and the **Half marathon** workflow) works like the Top 100 check and owns every `half-*.png` file; the default check owns every other file in the folder.
- **Captions:** `--captions` appends a "From the half (not numbered)" section to `docs/CHARTS_INSTAGRAM.md`.
- The pages link the images with "Save the image" (`components/half/HalfCharts.tsx` `SaveImage`, shown only when the image is in the build).

Adding the card sets changed `scripts/build-chart-cards.cjs`, which is in the Top 100 cards' code stamp, so on October 30, 2026 the hundred cards were re-rendered: their image data (IHDR and IDAT chunks) is byte-identical to the images of October 23, only the stamp chunk changed.

## Evidence

Measured October 23, 2026 on the build worktree, against `private-export-20261021-1050` (4,108,749 eligible finishes in 215 editions of 27 cities; 3,970,364 in the 200 editions the start-offset screen keeps; 1,067,423 with an exact recorded age in 33 granular-age editions):

- **The hundred:** finish times 15, pacing 16, racing 16, courses and weather 16, who runs 18, over the years 19; 13 of the 17 forms (bars 25, lines 17, dumbbell 11, big figure 9, columns 9, histogram 7, strip 6, waffle 4, range 4, heatmap 3, scatter 2, stacked 2, pyramid 1); weighting pooled 49, editions equal 34, pairs 17.
- **How they were chosen:** 135 tested candidates from six theme analysts, a curated 100 critiqued by three independent reviewers (visualisation, site rules, statistics; 187 points answered), eight weak or repeated entries replaced by a second round whose candidates two judges tried to refute (15 candidates, 4 refuted). Each chart was then built, reviewed by a visualisation editor and a data and rules auditor, fixed, polished and checked again.
- **The whole-set review:** four range reviewers and a series editor read every card, each finding checked by an independent skeptic: 103 findings, 77 confirmed (6 blockers, 20 major, 51 minor). The lead set the series style above; each module's owner applied its findings and an independent editor re-checked every card of the module, recomputing its accented numbers. Two near-duplicates were cut (the share within 10% of early pace at 35–40K, which repeated the fade heatmap; the coolest against the warmest morning, a third "warmer goes with more slowdowns" card), and the warmer-return card was upgraded to show where the time went. Their slots went to two analyses found in three further rounds of tested candidates scored by two judges (20 candidates): "Later slowdowns up front" and a New York 25–30K card.
- **The second whole-set review:** five readers again, each finding checked by an independent skeptic: 76 findings, 60 confirmed (3 blockers, 12 major, 45 minor). The blockers: a takeaway that read as a claim about the gender mix of the finishes under 3:00 (rewritten to the measured share of men's finishes); a back-of-the-field gap from percentiles pooled across editions (now measured within each edition, the typical edition's slowest 1% against its median); and "all 16 New York editions" when the release holds 18. Each module's owner fixed its findings and an independent editor re-checked every changed card. Two more cards were cut as weak or repeated (finish time against the 10K split, a restatement of the fade cards; the field's spread against its median finish, a loose scatter). Their slots went to the two highest-scored of ten fresh candidates, each tested by its analyst and then by two judges who tried to refute it: "Sub-3:30 doubles: the second more often slower" (two finishes of one linked record within three weeks; pairs banded by the mean of the two finishes, so regression to the mean is not built in, and compared with year-on pairs at the same ordered race pair: 44% quicker against 65% under 3:30) and "The 25–30K split ordered the field best" (each section ranked on its own: 72% of finishes within 5 places per 100 of their finish place, 43% on 35–40K, highest at 20–30K in 199 of 199 editions). The New York card was kept, both judges preferring it to the remaining candidates, and sharpened to speed-ups of 2% or more (41% against 5% in the typical race; every New York edition above every other race's typical edition). The series was then reordered (weak early cards later, strong buried ones earlier, New York, field-spread and 3:59 cards kept apart, no theme or form twice in a row).
- **Checks:** the full rebuild from the shards takes 367 s; `verify-charts` passes with 41,531 recount checks and 34,810 checks of the words (every guard holds, so every card shows its takeaway, not the fallback); `test_charts.py` passes (8 tests: every builder runs on the synthetic cohort or refuses with a message naming what is missing); `build-chart-cards.cjs --check` reports 100 current cards; on the dummy-key build the layout lint passes on `/charts` and the hundred pages at 195, 320, 390 and 1440 px (404 runs, no failing check), S5 and S8 pass (19 jobs), and `npm run build` writes 284 pages.
