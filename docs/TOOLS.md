# Runner tools

The `/tools` section has nine free marathon tools. Three are everyday utilities done carefully: a pace calculator, a finish-time predictor and a qualifying checker. Five use what only this dataset can show, because it has all nine 5 km checkpoints for every finish: a pace band, a race-day projector, a weather match, a course chooser and a split check. The ninth, the race planner (October 15, 2026; [below](#race-planner)), holds one course and one goal and goes from even pace to how finishes at that pace held up there, to the mat-by-mat comparison, to a printed pace band, then hands the course and goal to the other tools. Everything runs in the visitor's browser from static files. These tools are an addition: they do not change the release pins, the source-quality policy or any existing analysis output.

Since October 8, 2026 the section also has static reference pages written for what runners search for: two pace charts, five goal-time pages and one qualifying page per race ([below](#pace-charts-goal-pages-and-qualifying-race-pages)). They are not in the tools registry. Their search setup (sitemap, share cards, checks) is in [SEO](SEO.md#search-pages-october-2026).

## Why these tools

Research behind the selection (October 2026; Reddit and LetsRun refused automated reads, so community evidence came from Garmin forums, app-store reviews and running publications):

- Pace calculators and pace charts are the most used running tools. Predictors are second (built into watches and apps), then live race tracking. Pace bands, qualifying checkers and heat adjustments have smaller but committed audiences.
- Simple calculators are everywhere and copy the same formulas. The most common complaint is optimism: Riegel's formula predicts marathons a median of about 10 minutes too fast (Vickers & Vertosick 2016), and constant-pace projections ignore that most marathoners slow down.
- Weather is the factor calculators leave out. Published heat estimates disagree several-fold.

So the data tools answer questions constant-pace calculators cannot: what finishes that actually hit your goal ran at each mat, what similar finishes went on to do from your current mat, and how finishes at your pace held up on mornings like your forecast.

Rejected, and why:
- **A heat-adjusted goal time from Pace Notes data.** Weather is context only. Each edition has one modelled start-hour observation, not personal exposure, and cross-edition differences are confounded by course, field and era. The weather match is descriptive, with published estimates in a separate panel.
- **An automatic forecast pull.** This would add a third-party request and a data source outside the release pins. Visitors type their own forecast.
- **A fastest-course ranking or course time converter.** Cross-course differences are confounded. The course chooser shows descriptive same-pace outcomes, sorted alphabetically by default.
- **A grade-adjusted (GAP) pace band.** The supplied route profiles are current and of unknown historical validity; elevation stays context.
- **"Your chance" of a goal, BQ or cut-off.** Shares describe complete finishes; runners who stopped are not in the data.
- **Observed mile, halfway or cheer-spot splits between mats.** These would invent timing points. Even-pace rows are plain calculations and are described as such.

## Evidence kinds

Panels of Pace Notes data, published research or official standards carry one badge (`EvidencePanel` in `components/tools/ui.tsx`, drawn by `components/tools/EvidenceTag.tsx`). Since October 16, 2026 a badge is a short label that links to `/methodology#evidence` (a plain label inside a card that is itself a link), with no explanation beside it; what each kind means, and each tool's method, limits and sources (the old "How this works" boxes), are on `/methodology` (`#evidence`, `#tools-data`, `#tools-<slug>`). Plain calculations from the visitor's own inputs carry no badge: exact even-pace times, unit conversion, splits and targets. Copy calls them calculations. The site never uses the word "arithmetic", and `verify-tools` checks this.

Second pass (October 16, 2026; repository state, not deployed): author-year labels left the results (the predictor's rows read Riegel 1.06, Daniels–Gilbert, the half-to-full exponents, your two races and Tanda; the weather match names each study by who it measured, such as "Top finishers, seven marathons" for Ely et al. 2007). The split check's result text no longer states the 25% threshold or the final-stretch rule (the chart's 25% line and the step list keep their labels), and its pacing-type stat gives the type's share of finishes. Goal pages no longer rank their window's size, the course chooser's headline reads "How finishes at 3:30 pace held up on 22 courses" and its unpublished lists read "Too few finishes at this pace" and "Not published at any goal", the projector's group line drops its count, and the pace band and pace calculator drop the watch explanation. Each qualifying race page and checker card links its race's official qualifying page as plain functional copy ("Official qualifying page ↗"); the citations stay on `/methodology#tools-qualifying`. `verify-tools` bans the removed wording.

Shared code:
- `lib/tools/time.ts`: forgiving duration input. It accepts `3:30:00`, `3:30`, `3h30`, `210` (minutes) and keypad dots or commas (`8.05` is 8:05 for a pace, `8.5` and `8,5` are 8:30, `3.30` is 3:30 and `1.12.55` is 1:12:55 for a race time). It also parses clock times (`8.05 am` too) and tracker text.
- **Time fields and the keypad** (October 16, 2026). Time fields open the numeric keypad (`inputMode="decimal"`); on an iPhone it has a dot (a comma in some locales) and no colon, and no inputmode offers digits with a colon. So in every single race-time field (h:mm or h:mm:ss: the home form's goal, the tools' goals, finish, mat and checkpoint times, the predictor's and projector's times, the pace band's goal, the runner search's finish time, the stories' time boxes, and the analyses' and guide's time fields, which use the text keyboard) the dot or comma key types a colon as it is typed on a touch screen (`pointer: coarse`; a keyboard has a colon key, so there a typed dot stays a dot): `keypadColons` in `lib/keypad.ts` (re-exported by `lib/tools/time.ts`; a module of its own so the runner search and the home page, which load the time reader only when needed, stay small), applied by `keypadInput` in each field's change handler with the caret kept. Only the dot or comma key just pressed changes: pasted, dropped or deleted text and a keyboard's whole-word insert (a clipboard suggestion) are kept as they came, and so are their dots when the field is edited later. The field gets at most two group separators, counting dots already in it (a pasted `1.12` has one); a later dot is tenths (`1:12:55.3`) and a later comma shows a dot. Text that already has letters is left alone (`3.5h` or `9.14 am` arriving whole). Typed key by key, a dot becomes a colon before the next key arrives, so `parseDuration` reads the colons the keypad makes as the dots they were where only a dot makes sense: before a unit letter, where the colon comes before a single digit or before 60 or more (`3.5h` shows `3:5h`, `3.75 hours` shows `3:75 hours`, still 3.5 and 3.75 hours; a real clock with a unit reads as the clock, the unit naming its first group: `3:30h` is 3 h 30 min, `25:50 min` is 25 min 50 s) and before a single last digit in a race time (`45.5` shows `45:5`, still 45.5 minutes; a marathon goal typed `3.5` is 3.5 minutes and out of range, never 3:05), as before the change; a pace's colons are never read so. It also reads dots or commas before the last colon as group separators (`1.12:55` is 1:12:55). A short time with hundredths typed `25.50.12` shows `25:50:12`; the readers that take short times read it as 25 minutes 50.12 seconds where hours cannot be meant: the predictor when the hours reading is not a plausible pace for the distance (so a mile's `5:45` with tenths typed after it, `5:45:3`, is 5:45.3), the split check's nine boxes and the projector from 13 up (no mat is reached in under 13 minutes, and they read no time past 12 hours; the split check's pasted tracker text keeps its clocks, since a time-of-day column can read 13:26:31). In a single time box a comma is a dot (`24:53,4` is 24:53.4). Every other reader reads three groups as h:mm:ss, so a walker's `10.30.15` is 10:30:15. Pace fields (m:ss per mile or km) are not changed while typing, because `8.5` there is 8.5 minutes; `DurationField` shows m:ss when the field is left (`8.05` → 8:05, `8.5` → 8:30). Fields that take a list of times (the split check's and projector's paste boxes, the split check's and report's `s=` lists, whose commas separate the times) are never converted. Every reader behind a time field takes dots and commas between groups: `parseDuration`, `parseClock`, `readPlanGoal`, the split check's `parseMatTime` and `s=` reader, the report's `readReportTimes` (dots only: commas separate its nine times), the predictor's `parseRaceTime`, the projector's `parseElapsed`, `lib/personalized.ts` (`parseMinutes`, `parseElapsed`, `parseSection`) and the two stories' readers. The home form is a plain GET, so before or without its script a typed `3.30` is sent as `goal=3.30`, which the planner reads as 3:30 and rewrites to `goal=3:30`. Touch screens show "Tap . for :" with each race-time field that opens the keypad (`components/KeypadHint.tsx`, `.keypad-hint` in `app/globals.css`, shown by `@media (pointer: coarse)` from the first paint, so nothing moves): at the end of the field's help text, or as its own help line where a field has none, and once for the split check's nine boxes and the pacing-types story's nine. ### Links between tools

Every link from one tool to another follows the same rules:
- **Pace band:** `goal` is the whole minute at or below the time, as H:MM. `course` is added only when the pace band publishes that course, and `g` only when the visitor chose a recorded gender.
- **Course chooser:** `goal` is passed only within 2:30–6:30, the range it covers.
- **Projector:** gets the exact `target`, the `course` only when published, and `t=none`, so it waits for a tracker time instead of showing its example runner.
- **Qualifying checker:** never receives a predicted or goal time as if it had been run.
- **Units:** links carry the visitor's units, but only once the preference has been read. A link clicked before the page finishes loading falls back to the stored preference.
- **Race planner:** `planHref(city?, goalMinutes?)` opens `/tools/plan?course=<slug>&goal=H:MM` (either may be left out: all courses, the 4:00 example), and is null for a goal outside 1:30–8:00. The planner offers every course in `tools/course-goal.json` plus any whose editions were all screened out (29 today), so a course page or course-chooser row can always link to it. From the planner, each link uses its target's own form and names the course only where the target publishes it (read from the target's index at build time): the pace band `goal` as the planner holds it (H:MM, or H:MM:SS with seconds; the pace band reads both) and `course`; the projector `target=H:MM:SS&t=none` and `course`; the weather match `goal=H:MM:SS` and `course`; the course chooser `goal=H:MM` on its grid; `/courses/<slug>` where the page exists; the course's qualifying race page (`QUALIFYING_COURSE` in `lib/tools/qualifying.ts`); the pacing-pattern and hills-and-pacing analyses `race=<City>&goal=<minutes>` where the analyses take the city; the split check `course`.
- **Race report (October 16, 2026; repository state, not yet deployed):** under a typed race (not the made-up example), the split check's share row has "Make a race report": `reportHref(times, city?)` in `lib/tools/links.ts` opens `/report?s=<the same nine times>&course=<slug>`, with `g` added when a recorded gender is chosen (`reportLink` in `lib/report-shared.ts`). The page passes the client only course names, slugs and which tools and pages publish each course, read at build time from `tools/course-goal.json`, `tools/pace-band.json`, `tools/projector.json`, `tools/weather-match.json`, the course pages and the analyses' city list, plus the two index SHA-256 values. Results, in order, each `section` marked for `section_viewed` where noted (October 16, 2026: the definitions, citations, edition counts, "calculated from your goal" labels, source text and screen reasons described below left the page for `/methodology#tools-plan`; the sections, their order and their figures are unchanged):

1. **Your plan** (no badge): even pace per mile and per km and the elapsed time at each 5 km mat and the finish, calculated from the goal. A goal outside 1:30–8:00, or text that is not a time, shows "Pick a goal between 1:30 and 8:00." instead of results.
2. **On {course}, at {goal} pace** (`result`, Pace Notes data): the course chooser's row for that course at the nearest published goal (`publishedGoal`; "Results are published every 5 minutes; showing 3:30 for your 3:32."), with its finishes, editions, share under the goal, sustained-slowdown share (with the definition and citation), median time beyond the 5–20 km pace after 20 km and finish 10th/50th/90th percentiles, then the course's race months, start-temperature range, climb ("the sum of the 5 km sections of the supplied current route") and downhill-opening note, labelled context only. A course or goal without a row prints the reason the course chooser prints (the file's `unavailable[]` reason, too few editions, or every edition screened out, as for Prague), and the plan and pace band stay. All courses uses the pace band's all-courses group instead (finishes in the five minutes up to the goal), labelled as such.
3. **Where finishes near {goal} were at each mat** (`comparison`, Pace Notes data): the pace band's course (or all-courses) group for all recorded genders at the whole minute at or below the goal, median elapsed time at each mat for held pace and for sustained slowdown, with finishes and editions, beside the even-pace column labelled "Your plan". A group under 100 finishes is not shown; with neither group, or no pace band shard (Melbourne, Prague), the panel is left out. Under it, a closed "Same goal on every course (alphabetical)" list of the published rows at that goal, the chosen course marked, and a link to the course chooser.
4. **Your pace band** (`pace-band`, no badge): `PaceBandStrip`, the pace band's own strips (mile or km strips by the units on screen, the "H" line from step 3's held-pace group), one row of "Copy link", "Print wristband strips" and "Print this plan" (`ShareBar` with the two print buttons as its `extra`), and "Open the full pace band".
5. **Before race day**: the weather match, the race-day projector and the course page; the course's official qualifying page, with the qualifying checker's Official standards badge, in its own group (the six races in `QUALIFYING_COURSE`); the pacing-pattern and hills-and-pacing analyses.
6. **After the race**: the split check, "Paste your nine splits for a report you can print, save or share".

**Printing.** "Print this plan" (and the browser's own print command) prints one Letter or A4 page at the browser's default margins, in miles or km: a header with the course, goal and even pace (the chosen unit first), the cut-out strips (orange cut edges) without their own heading line, the mat table (even pace beside the observed medians, with finishes and editions, under the panel's title and its one-line description of the window; even pace alone where there is no observed group) and a source line: "Your plan beside Pace Notes data · splithappens.run/tools/plan" when observed columns print, or "Your plan, calculated from your goal · splithappens.run/tools/plan" when the page holds only calculated times (October 16, 2026, after review: a calculation-only page is not attributed to the data). The pace band panel carries the Pace Notes data badge whenever its strips show the observed "H" line. The data badge and the caption under the table are left off the page: the table's "Pace Notes data" group heading marks the observed columns. "Print wristband strips" prints the strips alone, with their heading line. Navigation, inputs, cards, links and error boxes are not printed; text is ink on white. The km plan with a comparison is the tallest (four km strips): measured on October 16, 2026 as Chromium PDFs, it has about 1.2 cm to spare on Letter at 0.4 in margins and about 3 cm on A4 at 1 cm, and it also fits at 0.5 in.

**Analytics.** Nothing new: `tool_used` `edit` (the workspace), `print` (both buttons, through `beforeprint`) and `copy_link`; `section_viewed` `result`, `comparison` and `pace-band`; `site_error` `data_fetch` from `dataFetch`. `SectionTracker` looks for marked sections once per page view, so the three stay mounted from the first render, each hidden while it has nothing to show (the comparison until its data loads, or for a course without a pace band group), and count when they first show; a comparison that appeared after the page loaded was never counted in the first browser run. The course and goal are never sent; the inputs are masked and the results left out of recordings like every tool's.

**Checks.** `verify-tools` checks `planHref`, the goal reading, the nearest-goal rule at both ends of the grid, that every published course-goal row is the one the planner shows for its course and goal, the Prague and too-few-editions reasons (Melbourne under 0934; since 1652 every course with too few editions, Barcelona, Paris and Singapore), the gap and out-of-range reasons, the pace band cell beside it, and the first rendered plan (the 4:00 example: 9:09/mi, 5:41/km and each mat's time, the tracked sections, the links in each target's form). It recounts the planner's worked example, the Berlin 3:30 row, from the checksum-verified runner records: finishes, editions, share under the goal, sustained-slowdown share, time after 20 km and the three finish percentiles (`verify-insights` recounts every row's finishes, editions and shares, but not the times). The browser checks type a goal and choose a course in two runs (S2 `plan`, `plan-url`, Berlin against Rotterdam: neither the goal nor the course reaches PostHog). Both courses are ones the page never names by itself: a value the other run also shows is treated as page content, so since October 16, 2026 S2 fails when a case's own value turns up in the other run's traffic (Chicago, the first choice, is named in the method note's duplicated fields, so its check could never fail). S6 checks the three sections in order (since the October 17, 2026 redesign the shorter page also lets the chapter end report "related" once after them), both print buttons, Copy link and the copied address.

- without the start-offset editions (15 under 1652; 13 under 0934);

### Rebuilding

```bash
python3 analysis/build_insights.py --only tool-projector,tool-pace-band,tool-weather-match,tool-course-goal --output public/data/insights
node scripts/verify-insights.cjs
```

Rebuilding with `--only` keeps every other family's file and rewrites the manifest. The manifest binds every `insights_*.py` module's SHA-256, so editing a tool builder without rebuilding fails the verifier.

## Verification

- `scripts/verify-insights.cjs` independently recounts each tool family from the release shards. It checks:
  - the tool cohort;
  - every published projector cell's finishes and editions, and that no 100+ group is missing;
  - exact percentiles, slowdown splits and remaining pace for every All-courses cell at 20 and 30 km;
  - every pace-band group count, with sampled medians and slowdown shares;
  - the weather editions, rows, matched editions and edition-balanced shares;
  - the course-goal rows, the unavailable list and the course list.
- `scripts/verify-tools.cjs` (in `npm run verify:data`) checks golden values (225 on October 8, 2026): parsing, split tables, the pace charts' even-pace values recomputed by hand, the goal-page list, slugs and links, Riegel and Daniels values, Tanda's range, heat formulas against published tables, age rules and standards for every race, the Boston downhill index and cut-off history, and split reading; since October 15, 2026 (306 checks) also the result handoffs: each link's form and range against what the pace band and `readAnalysisProfile` read, and the rendered cards; and since the race planner (371 checks, about 16 s, most of it reading the runner records) the planner's link, goal reading and course-goal rows, with one row recounted from the records ([above](#race-planner)).
- `analysis/test_insights.py` (`Tools` class) covers:
  - the slug rule;
  - projector bands, trend variants and the slowdown split;
  - the pace-band window boundary (3:55:00 is not in the 3:55 window);
  - the weather three-edition gate and edition balancing;
  - the course-goal tolerance and gates.

## Privacy

Tools compute in the browser and send no typed values to any server (the course and recorded-gender choices only select which public data file is fetched from the site's host); session replays mask what is typed and leave out `.tool-results` panels and the gender or division choice (see [ANALYTICS.md](ANALYTICS.md)). A new tool must render its inputs inside `.tool-workspace` and its results inside `.tool-results`, which `scripts/verify-analytics.cjs` checks. Tool state is kept in the URL so a visitor can share a link: goals, paces, splits, course and units. Typing updates the URL with `history.replaceState`, which makes no request. The site's referrer policy is `strict-origin` (root `metadata.referrer`), and `loadInsight` fetches data with `referrerPolicy: 'no-referrer'`, so later requests never carry the query string. Opening a shared link does send that URL to the host, like any page request. A birth date is never in the URL. The qualifying checker keeps it in component state, plus `localStorage` only if the visitor ticks "remember on this device". Projector runner cards and the start-line clock are stored only in `localStorage`; the start clock expires after 12 hours, and card labels never go into links. Analytics records only the tool's path (`/tools/<slug>`); `lib/analytics-policy.ts` and `lib/replay-policy.ts` strip every query string and fragment from events and recordings, and `scripts/verify-analytics.cjs` tests that tool inputs are removed.

## Known limitations

- **Referrer on first load:** the referrer policy is a `<meta>` tag, so on its own it would let the CSS and JavaScript requested before it is parsed send the page URL to the same host. On Vercel, `vercel.json` also sends an HTTP `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin` header on every path, which closes that gap. A build served without that header (the optional GitHub Pages build) still sends the URL on those first requests, to the host that already received it with the page request.
- **Projector shard size:** the All-courses projector shards are about 80–95 KB gzipped, because they include the men and women variants. Splitting those into separate files would make each smaller.
- **Course chooser temperatures:** a course's start-temperature range covers all of its editions, not only the editions in the row, and the page labels it that way.
- **Projector at 40 km:** the projector is no more accurate than even pace at 40 km (see the accuracy table), and says so on the page.
- **Boston window end:** the end of Boston 2028's qualifying window (2027 registration week) is not yet dated. The checker treats times after September 30, 2027 as outside it and flags September 2027.

## Annual maintenance: qualifying standards

Qualifying standards and windows change every year. `lib/tools/qualifying.ts` records `VERIFIED_AT` (currently 2026-10-08), and every checker card and race page shows it. The review covers the checker and the race pages (`/tools/qualifying/<race>`) together: both read the same `STANDARDS`, and the race pages' sitemap `lastmod` is `VERIFIED_AT`. Before each registration season, and at least every September:

1. **Boston.** Check the standards for the next edition, the qualifying window start, the age date (race day), and the downhill-course rule (the index thresholds in `bostonDownhillIndex`). After registration closes, append the new cut-off, field and not-accepted counts to `BOSTON_CUTOFFS` from the B.A.A. announcement.
2. **New York.** Check the time-qualifier bands, which NYRR races count, the age rule and the application window.
3. **London Good For Age.** Check the bands, the window, the strictly-under rule, the UK-residency rule and the application dates.
4. **Chicago.** Check the bands, including the youngest band's range, the window and the application dates.
5. **Berlin.** Check the birth-year bands and registration dates.
6. **Sydney.** Check the High Performance Program standards and window.
7. Update `sources`, then `VERIFIED_AT`. Update the golden values in `scripts/verify-tools.cjs` and run `npm run verify:data`.
8. Never forecast a cut-off. The cut-off table shows only past cut-offs the visitor's margin would have cleared.
9. **Race pages.** Open each `/tools/qualifying/<race>` page in a build. Check the title and heading year (read from `edition`), the at-a-glance card, the window end (an application deadline or an undated end is shown as such), the entry text and the per-race words in `components/tools/QualifyingRaceText.tsx` (organiser, entry route, page noun, search name). A race added to `STANDARDS` gets a page, a sitemap entry and a share card from that list, but also needs those word maps and its course in `QUALIFYING_COURSE` (`lib/tools/qualifying.ts`). If a race name changes, run `npm run og:images` and commit the new card. Then `npm run build` and `npm run verify:seo`.

Dated application windows show an "open now / upcoming / closed" badge. Where a race states a time of day (London 16:00 GMT; Chicago 8 a.m. and 2 p.m. CT), `opensAt` and `closesAt` hold the UTC instant and the badge compares it with the current time. Otherwise it uses the visitor's calendar date. A stale entry looks closed rather than wrong, but it still needs this review.

Points checked against official pages on 2026-10-07:
- **Boston:** the downhill index uses the B.A.A.'s own metric bounds for drops typed in metres: 457.2, 914.2 and 1,828.6 m. These are not exact conversions of 1,500, 3,000 and 6,000 ft. The 2028 race date (April 17) is confirmed. The end of the qualifying window, 2027 registration week, is not yet dated.
- **Time equal to the standard:** New York ("at least as fast as") and Chicago ("within the standards") state that an equal time qualifies. Boston, Berlin and Sydney do not, and the checker says it assumes an equal time counts. London requires strictly under.
- **New York:** NYRR guaranteed entry needs a time from the 2026 TCS New York City Marathon (November 1, 2026) or a listed NYRR half. The pool cut-offs (2025: 13:20, top 25%; 2026: 22:52, top 10%) are both from NYRR press releases.

## Adding a tool

1. Add an entry to `lib/tools/registry.ts` with its evidence kinds and, for data tools, its index file.
2. Create `app/tools/<slug>/page.tsx` (server: metadata, `ToolHeader`, the client component, `ToolNext`) and `components/tools/<Component>.tsx`. Put the tool's method, limits and sources on `/methodology` (`components/methods/MethodsTools.tsx`, a `Detail` with id `tools-<slug>`), never on the tool page; badges go through `EvidenceTag`.
3. Label every panel with `EvidencePanel`. Follow the repository contracts in `AGENTS.md`: say "sustained slowdown" with the citation; observed shares, never "your chance"; no data-based weather or elevation adjustment; no invented splits; groups of at least 100 finishes.
4. For a new data family, add an `insights_tool_<name>.py` module, register it in `optional_families()` with a `tool-` prefix, add a recount to `scripts/verify-insights.cjs` and the module to `toolModules`, and add tests.
5. Put every form field inside `.tool-workspace` and every result inside `.tool-results`, so session replays mask the inputs and leave the results out. Wrap any goal-dependent text outside those (headings, aria-labels, gender or division choices) in `ph-no-capture`.
6. Add the path to analytics automatically through the registry, and run `npm run verify:data`, `npm run verify:analytics`, `npm run build` and the Python tests.
