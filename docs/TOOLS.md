# Runner tools

The `/tools` section has nine free marathon tools. Three are everyday utilities done carefully: a pace calculator, a finish-time predictor and a qualifying checker. Five use what only this dataset can show, because it has all nine 5 km checkpoints for every finish: a pace band, a race-day projector, a weather match, a course chooser and a split check. The ninth, the race planner (October 15, 2026; [below](#race-planner)), holds one course and one goal and goes from the plan (spread over the course's climbs and descents where its route profile is checked, the [terrain splits](#terrain-splits-owner-decision-october-23-2026) of October 23, 2026, which replaced the October 20 [course shape](#course-shape-band-owner-decision-october-20-2026--superseded-october-23-2026); even pace otherwise) to how finishes at that pace held up there, to the mat-by-mat comparison, to a printed pace band, then hands the course and goal to the other tools. Everything runs in the visitor's browser from static files. These tools are an addition: they do not change the release pins, the source-quality policy or any existing analysis output.

Since October 8, 2026 the section also has static reference pages written for what runners search for: two pace charts, five goal-time pages and one qualifying page per race ([below](#pace-charts-goal-pages-and-qualifying-race-pages)). They are not in the tools registry. Their search setup (sitemap, share cards, checks) is in [SEO](SEO.md#search-pages-october-2026).

## Why these tools

Research behind the selection (October 2026; Reddit and LetsRun refused automated reads, so community evidence came from Garmin forums, app-store reviews and running publications):

- Pace calculators and pace charts are the most used running tools. Predictors are second (built into watches and apps), then live race tracking. Pace bands, qualifying checkers and heat adjustments have smaller but committed audiences.
- Simple calculators are everywhere and copy the same formulas. The most common complaint is optimism: Riegel's formula predicts marathons a median of about 10 minutes too fast (Vickers & Vertosick 2016), and constant-pace projections ignore that most marathoners slow down.
- Weather is the factor calculators leave out. Published heat estimates disagree several-fold.

So the data tools answer questions constant-pace calculators cannot: what finishes that actually hit your goal ran at each mat, what similar finishes went on to do from your current mat, and how finishes at your pace held up on mornings like your forecast.

Rejected, and why:
- **A heat-adjusted goal time from Pace Notes data.** Weather is context only. Each edition has one modelled start-hour observation, not personal exposure, and cross-edition differences are confounded by course, field and era. The weather match is descriptive, with published estimates in a separate panel.
- **An automatic forecast pull.** This would add a third-party request and a data source outside the release pins. Visitors type their own forecast.
- **A fastest-course ranking or course time converter.** Cross-course differences are confounded. The course chooser shows descriptive same-pace outcomes, sorted alphabetically by default.
- **A grade-adjusted (GAP) pace band.** Rejected on October 20, 2026 (the supplied route profiles were uncorrected, and the data cannot say what a hill costs a runner, so the band took the course's observed shape instead: the [course-shape band](#course-shape-band-owner-decision-october-20-2026--superseded-october-23-2026)); superseded on October 23, 2026 by the [terrain splits](#terrain-splits-owner-decision-october-23-2026), which spread the visitor's own goal over a corrected profile and adjust no finish time.
- **"Your chance" of a goal, BQ or cut-off.** Shares describe complete finishes; runners who stopped are not in the data.
- **Observed mile, halfway or cheer-spot splits between mats.** These would invent timing points. Even-pace rows are plain calculations and are described as such. Terrain splits' mile, km and halfway rows are points on a plan from published research, never observed timings. (From October 20 to 23, 2026 the course-shape band's rows held each segment's pace evenly between the nine observed timing points.)

## Evidence kinds

Panels of Pace Notes data, published research or official standards carry one badge (`EvidencePanel` in `components/tools/ui.tsx`, drawn by `components/tools/EvidenceTag.tsx`). Since October 16, 2026 a badge is a short label that links to `/methodology#evidence` (a plain label inside a card that is itself a link), with no explanation beside it; what each kind means, and each tool's method, limits and sources (the old "How this works" boxes), are on `/methodology` (`#evidence`, `#tools-data`, `#tools-<slug>`). Plain calculations from the visitor's own inputs carry no badge: exact even-pace times, unit conversion, splits and targets. Copy calls them calculations. The site never uses the word "arithmetic", and `verify-tools` checks this.

Second pass (October 16, 2026; repository state, not deployed): author-year labels left the results (the predictor's rows read Riegel 1.06, Daniels–Gilbert, the half-to-full exponents, your two races and Tanda; the weather match names each study by who it measured, such as "Top finishers, seven marathons" for Ely et al. 2007). The split check's result text no longer states the 25% threshold or the final-stretch rule (the chart's 25% line and the step list keep their labels), and its pacing-type stat gives the type's share of finishes. Goal pages no longer rank their window's size, the course chooser's headline reads "How finishes at 3:30 pace held up on 22 courses" and its unpublished lists read "Too few finishes at this pace" and "Not published at any goal", the projector's group line drops its count, and the pace band and pace calculator drop the watch explanation. Each qualifying race page and checker card links its race's official qualifying page as plain functional copy ("Official qualifying page ↗"); the citations stay on `/methodology#tools-qualifying`. `verify-tools` bans the removed wording.

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |

Shared code:
- `lib/tools/time.ts`: forgiving duration input. It accepts `3:30:00`, `3:30`, `3h30`, `210` (minutes) and keypad dots or commas (`8.05` is 8:05 for a pace, `8.5` and `8,5` are 8:30, `3.30` is 3:30 and `1.12.55` is 1:12:55 for a race time). It also parses clock times (`8.05 am` too) and tracker text.
- **Time fields and the keypad** (October 16, 2026). Time fields open the numeric keypad (`inputMode="decimal"`); on an iPhone it has a dot (a comma in some locales) and no colon, and no inputmode offers digits with a colon. So in every single race-time field (h:mm or h:mm:ss: the home form's goal, the tools' goals, finish, mat and checkpoint times, the predictor's and projector's times, the pace band's goal, the runner search's finish time, the stories' time boxes, and the analyses' and guide's time fields, which use the text keyboard) the dot or comma key types a colon as it is typed on a touch screen (`pointer: coarse`; a keyboard has a colon key, so there a typed dot stays a dot): `keypadColons` in `lib/keypad.ts` (re-exported by `lib/tools/time.ts`; a module of its own so the runner search and the home page, which load the time reader only when needed, stay small), applied by `keypadInput` in each field's change handler with the caret kept. Only the dot or comma key just pressed changes: pasted, dropped or deleted text and a keyboard's whole-word insert (a clipboard suggestion) are kept as they came, and so are their dots when the field is edited later. The field gets at most two group separators, counting dots already in it (a pasted `1.12` has one); a later dot is tenths (`1:12:55.3`) and a later comma shows a dot. Text that already has letters is left alone (`3.5h` or `9.14 am` arriving whole). Typed key by key, a dot becomes a colon before the next key arrives, so `parseDuration` reads the colons the keypad makes as the dots they were where only a dot makes sense: before a unit letter, where the colon comes before a single digit or before 60 or more (`3.5h` shows `3:5h`, `3.75 hours` shows `3:75 hours`, still 3.5 and 3.75 hours; a real clock with a unit reads as the clock, the unit naming its first group: `3:30h` is 3 h 30 min, `25:50 min` is 25 min 50 s) and before a single last digit in a race time (`45.5` shows `45:5`, still 45.5 minutes; a marathon goal typed `3.5` is 3.5 minutes and out of range, never 3:05), as before the change; a pace's colons are never read so. It also reads dots or commas before the last colon as group separators (`1.12:55` is 1:12:55). A short time with hundredths typed `25.50.12` shows `25:50:12`; the readers that take short times read it as 25 minutes 50.12 seconds where hours cannot be meant: the predictor when the hours reading is not a plausible pace for the distance (so a mile's `5:45` with tenths typed after it, `5:45:3`, is 5:45.3), the split check's nine boxes and the projector from 13 up (no mat is reached in under 13 minutes, and they read no time past 12 hours; the split check's pasted tracker text keeps its clocks, since a time-of-day column can read 13:26:31). In a single time box a comma is a dot (`24:53,4` is 24:53.4). Every other reader reads three groups as h:mm:ss, so a walker's `10.30.15` is 10:30:15. Pace fields (m:ss per mile or km) are not changed while typing, because `8.5` there is 8.5 minutes; `DurationField` shows m:ss when the field is left (`8.05` → 8:05, `8.5` → 8:30). Fields that take a list of times (the split check's and projector's paste boxes, the split check's and report's `s=` lists, whose commas separate the times) are never converted. Every reader behind a time field takes dots and commas between groups: `parseDuration`, `parseClock`, `readPlanGoal`, the split check's `parseMatTime` and `s=` reader, the report's `readReportTimes` (dots only: commas separate its nine times), the predictor's `parseRaceTime`, the projector's `parseElapsed`, `lib/personalized.ts` (`parseMinutes`, `parseElapsed`, `parseSection`) and the two stories' readers. The home form is a plain GET, so before or without its script a typed `3.30` is sent as `goal=3.30`, which the planner reads as 3:30 and rewrites to `goal=3:30`. Touch screens show "Tap . for :" with each race-time field that opens the keypad (`components/KeypadHint.tsx`, `.keypad-hint` in `app/globals.css`, shown by `@media (pointer: coarse)` from the first paint, so nothing moves): at the end of the field's help text, or as its own help line where a field has none, and once for the split check's nine boxes and the pacing-types story's nine. It is `aria-hidden` (a typed colon or dot both work, so nothing needs announcing) and the labels are unchanged. Checks: `verify-tools` (the helper, the caret, paste, whole-word and keyboard rules, `25.50.12` and `10.30.15` in each reader of short times, `45.5` and `3.5` typed key by key, unit text typed key by key (`3.5h`, `3.75 hours`, `45.5 min`) read as it was before the change, and each reader with `3.30`, `3.30.15`, `1.12.55`, `8.05`, `8.5` and rejected nonsense, and `readPlanGoal('3.30')`); S1's `keypad colon` job (on the phone: the hint shows and is hidden from screen readers, `3.30` typed in the home goal shows 3:30 and the planner opens at 3:30, the pace calculator's time shows 3:30:15 as 3.30.15 is typed, its pace keeps 8.5 and shows 8:30 when left, one hint for the split check's boxes, and at 195 px wide (200% zoom) the pace band's inline hint stays on one line; on a desktop the hint is not shown, a typed dot stays a dot, and `3.5h` and `3.75 hours` typed key by key in the planner read 3:30:00 and 3:45:00) and its home form job (`goal=3.30` without JavaScript); S2 types the home goal, the split check's nine times and the predictor's training pace with dots, so its check that typed values never reach PostHog covers them.

### Links between tools

Every link from one tool to another follows the same rules:
- **Pace band:** `goal` is the whole minute at or below the time, as H:MM. `course` is added only when the pace band publishes that course, and `g` only when the visitor chose a recorded gender.
- **Course chooser:** `goal` is passed only within 2:30–6:30, the range it covers.
- **Projector:** gets the exact `target`, the `course` only when published, and `t=none`, so it waits for a tracker time instead of showing its example runner.
- **Qualifying checker:** never receives a predicted or goal time as if it had been run.

## Terrain splits (owner decision, October 23, 2026)

**The feedback and the decision.** The owner relayed a visitor's message about the planner (recorded here on October 23, 2026), verbatim:

> I plugged in my hoped for 5:15 finish in New York next weekend to see what showed up. The "course shape" data indicates a pretty severe fade in the second half, so those are not splits I would want to emulate. I think people should be forewarned about using them since they are based on the reality that most runners fade during the second half. What would be useful would be splits based on course terrain. I am not planning on running even pace over the 59th St. Bridge.

The owner's instructions, verbatim: "figure it out and fix", then, on October 23, 2026, "It should use an elevation model". This supersedes the October 20, 2026 decision against an elevation model ([below](#course-shape-band-owner-decision-october-20-2026--superseded-october-23-2026)) for the planner's and the pace band's band only. Every other elevation rule stands: supplied-route elevation is context everywhere else, never a personal-exposure measurement and never a finish-time adjustment (`AGENTS.md` records the one exception), and the course pages, share cards, `course-geometry.json` and the all-finisher analyses still use the supplied profiles (open items below). The race the visitor asked about, the New York City Marathon, is on Sunday, November 1, 2026.

**What changes.** On ten courses with a checked route profile (Amsterdam, Austin, Boston, Chicago, Eindhoven, London, Madrid, New York, Rotterdam, Valencia) the band's default becomes **Terrain**: the visitor's own goal spread over the course's corrected elevation profile at an even effort with one published grade curve. It ends exactly on the goal (the goal is never changed), does not depend on recorded gender or on any finish data, and carries the Published research badge. **Even pace** is the only alternative. Every other course, and All courses, is even pace; on a course, one line says it has no terrain splits (the design had it say why; since October 29, 2026 the reason is only on `/methodology`). The as-run course shape (the median of finishes that made the goal, scaled to it) is retired: it is no longer a mode and is never printed, drawn on a strip or scaled. The observed medians near the goal survive only unscaled, in a Pace Notes data panel, with the forewarning the visitor asked for ("As run, slowdowns included: 48% had a sustained slowdown. Not splits to copy." at New York 5:15). The held-pace "H" line leaves the wristband in both modes. No runner-record statistic is added, and the pace-band shards and every release pin are untouched.

**The rule** (`analysis/terrain_model.py`; the normative reference is `analysis/course_terrain/validation-2026-10-23/terrain_model.py`, which the port reproduces bit for bit on all 15 corrected profiles):
- **Curve.** Pace on a grade divided by pace on the flat at equal effort: f(g) = 1 + A1·g + A2·g², A1 = 0.02869556, A2 = 0.001520768, g the grade in percent, clamped to −10% … +10% before f is applied. f(+2) = 1.0635, f(−2) = 0.9487, f(+4) = 1.1391, f(−4) = 0.9096, f(+10) = 1.4390, f(−10) = 0.8651, minimum 0.8646 at −9.43%. (The October 23 design listed f(−10) = 0.8650; the formula gives 0.86512.)
- **Sources.** Robb D, "An Improved GAP Model", Strava Engineering, 3 October 2017 (equal heart rate on the flat; it replaced Strava's Minetti-based model), method in US Patent 11,623,121 B1, which gives no coefficients. The coefficients are a third party's quadratic fit to the chart in that post (sci-dani.com, "Mind the GAP"), cross-checked against Fellrnr's printed approximation (within 0.004 on −10% … +10%, the largest gap 0.0033 at −10%, and within 0.002 from −6% to +10%, once its linear term's sign is read as +; `analysis/course_terrain/validation-2026-10-23/curve_crosscheck.py` and its `curve_crosscheck.json`, committed October 29, 2026, reproducing the design work's table). The Strava post itself returned HTTP 403 and was not read directly, so `/methodology` says the coefficients are a third party's fit. Not used, and cited as the reason: Minetti et al. 2002 (doi:10.1152/japplphysiol.01177.2001; its own abstract: predicted downhill speeds "well above those attained in downhill competitions"). Supporting, abstract only: Townshend, Worringham & Stewart 2010 (doi:10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181af21e2), cited only as "runners chose to spend less effort downhill", never as agreeing with the curve. The peer-reviewed alternative, named on `/methodology`: Kay 2012 (doi:10.1515/1559-0410.1456).
- **Pipeline** (one `u` per course, built once by `analysis/build_course_terrain.py`): the corrected route points, rounded to 0.001 km and 0.01 m and published as the model's only input (`route`); stretched linearly to 42.195 km; a grid of exactly 4,221 points (10 m); linear interpolation; a 21-sample running median and a Gaussian of σ = 10 samples (kernel ±40), both with nearest-value edges; grade by central differences (one-sided at the ends); trapezoid weights w = ½(f(gᵢ) + f(gᵢ₊₁))·Δx; U = the cumulative weights divided by their total, so U ends at exactly 1. The file stores `u` at every km (42), every mile (26) and halfway, to 7 decimals.
- **On the page.** Each marker's elapsed time is round(goal × u); the finish is the goal exactly; a split is the difference of two rounded elapsed times, so splits always sum to the goal. Whole-unit splits for the climbs line come from consecutive whole-unit elapsed times. The multiplier does not depend on pace, so one `u` serves every goal and every recorded gender.

- **The rule.** The stretches are the nine 5K sections (`0–5K` … `35–40K`, then `40K–Finish`, 2.195 km) and every whole mile 1–26 then the last 0.2188 mi (`Last 0.2 mi`); on a km band every km 1–42 then the last 0.195 km (`Last 0.2 km`). ## Course-shape band (owner decision, October 20, 2026) — superseded October 23, 2026

**Superseded.** On October 23, 2026 the [terrain splits](#terrain-splits-owner-decision-october-23-2026) replaced this band: the as-run course shape is no longer a mode, is never printed or scaled, and its medians survive only unscaled in the as-run panel. This section is kept as the record of the October 20 work (merged in pull request #76 as `659d2c0`), with its stale details corrected in place.

**The question and the decision.** The owner asked on October 20, 2026: "For the pace band in plan your race the paces are the same no matter what race…is that right? Shouldn't it change based on elevation of the course and how most people on each course run that pace?" The answer: no elevation model (supplied-route elevation stays context, never a finish-time adjustment, and the data cannot say what a hill costs a runner), but the course's observed shape, which the published pace-band data already holds per course. The owner then decided: "Have the band switch to course shape". This supersedes the earlier stance that the planner's band is only the visitor's even pace ("The planner never suggests a pace" on `/methodology`, now replaced). Repository state: branch `feat/course-shape-band`, not pushed, merged or deployed when written. No data, `analysis/**`, `public/data/**` or pin changed.

**The rule** (`lib/tools/course-shape.ts`, one function for both pages and the verifier):
- The group is the existing pace-band shard for the course and recorded gender (`paceBandShardPath`, `cellOf`; the planner uses all recorded genders, the pace band the gender chosen), group `all`: every screened finish from G − 5:00 to G − 0:01, G the whole minute at or below the goal, with `e50` the median elapsed time at each of the nine official timing points (the 5–40 km mats and the finish).
- Each `e50` is multiplied by goal ÷ `e50[finish]`, so the band ends exactly on the goal (seconds kept, 3:32:30 ends on 3:32:30) and keeps the course's proportions.
- Between timing points each segment's pace is held evenly: the mile and km rows, and halfway on those strips, are interpolated, never presented as observed.
- A shape exists only where `cellOf` returns a cell (100 or more finishes) whose finishes come from at least `SHAPE_MIN_EDITIONS` (3) editions, the course results' `min_editions` (owner decision, October 20, 2026, verbatim: "Do whatever you reccomend", on the recommendation to require 3 editions: one or two race days describe their own weather and fields more than the course). So Singapore (one screened edition), Barcelona and Paris (two), and from the October 21, 2026 release Auckland (two, after its 2017 edition was held), had no course shape at any goal, and Helsinki 4:27 (a group on two of its three editions) has none at that goal; each says so in one line ("Too few editions in Singapore for a course shape.", "Too few editions at this goal in Helsinki for a course shape."). A thin course is known from the index at build time (`shapeGoalsOf` keeps each course's `editions`), so it settles at once as even pace with no wait; `/methodology#tools-course-shape` names the courses from the index.
- Where there is no shape the band is even pace. On the planner one line in the choice's place says why (`noShapeReason`, `noShapeLine`, never a count nobody made): "No course shape for Prague." (no pace-band group: its editions were screened out), "Course shapes cover goals from 2:30 to 6:30." (a goal outside the index's published goals, such as New York City 7:00 or Boston 2:29, whatever the window holds), or "Too few finishes at this goal in {city} for a course shape." (an in-range goal whose group has no cell). On the pace band the Splits field shows nothing there: its finishes section, directly below, already says why (not published for this goal, course or selection, or outside the observed range), so the reason is said once. All courses pooled is even pace, with no line and no choice (there is no course to shape it).
- **Default and override:** a course with a shape opens in course shape; "Splits: Course shape | Even pace" (`components/tools/BandChoice.tsx`, the site's segmented `Choice`) switches it. Even pace is kept in the address as `pace=even` through `useQueryState`, so links, reload and back/forward reproduce it; course shape, the default, stays out. Once the page has read its address, `tidyChoice` drops a hand-edited value (`pace=foo`, `pace=EVEN`, `pace=course`) and drops `pace=even` on All courses (no choice there), so neither stays in the address or the copied link; a choice of even pace made on one course carries to the next. Where there is no shape the choice is not offered. Under 300 px (200% zoom) each option's label wraps inside its own button.
- **While a group loads:** the choice keeps its place, and the card and strips take the course shape's layout with dashes for times (the finish is the goal), dimmed and `aria-busy`, never the even-pace figures under a pressed Course shape; the card keeps its height when the shape lands. Printing waits (the print buttons are disabled; Copy link stays, the address being already right). On the pace band a shape that is reloading is kept only for the same course and recorded gender, so a new gender's labels never sit on another group's times.

**Data and sources.** The pace charts use no data: `chartRows()` spreads each goal evenly over 42.195 km or 21.0975 km (a mile is 1.609344 km), rounding to whole seconds once, at display. The goal pages' observed panels read the verified pace-band family (`tools/pace-band.json` and its All courses, all recorded genders shard, the same groups the pace band shows) and the bunching panel reads `finish-times.json`, the data behind the 3:59 effect story; if the pace-band files are not in the verified manifest or the shard digest does not match, the observed panel says the mat times are not available in this build and the other observed panels are left out; the bunching panel is left out when `finish-times.json` is missing. Counts are finishes in the tool cohort, editions pooled; there are no observed halfway or mile splits, because the source records only the 5 km mats. The goal pages cite the published slowdown method (`SLOWDOWN_CITATION`). The race pages read only `lib/tools/qualifying.ts` and cite each race's own `sources`. The per-race words (organiser, entry route, London's "Good For Age times", the search name "NYC Marathon") are maps in `QualifyingRaceText.tsx`, and the course link is `QUALIFYING_COURSE` in `lib/tools/qualifying.ts` (it was `COURSE_CITY` in the route until October 15, 2026; the race planner links back through its reverse, `qualifyingKeyForCity`).

**Why only five goal pages.** One page per minute would be near-duplicate pages. To add a goal, add it to `GOAL_PAGE_MINUTES` (inside the pace band's 2:30–6:30 range): the page, the sitemap entry and the share card follow from that list. Update the goal-page assertions in `scripts/verify-tools.cjs` and run `npm run og:images`.

## Data families

The builders are `analysis/insights_tool_*.py`, run by `analysis/build_insights.py` as optional families (the terrain file, last in the table, has its own builder and uses no finishes). All four cohort families use the tool cohort from `analysis/insights_tools_common.py`:
- the story cohort (under 0934 without the duplicate Chicago 2018–19; since 1652 the duplicate screen is empty);
- without the start-offset editions (15 under 1652; 13 under 0934);
- without the editions whose mat grid looks shifted (3 under 1652: Madrid 2023, Valencia 2021 and Warsaw 2022; 2 under 0934).

This is the final-kick cohort: **3,710,359 finishes in 193 editions** from `htw-db-2026-10-16-1652` (October 16, 2026; branch `feat/release-1652`, not deployed; 3,260,661 in 179 editions under 0934). Other counts on this page are the 0934 build's unless marked; the published tool files are authoritative. Every published group has at least 100 finishes. Counts are finishes, not runners.

### Rebuilding

```bash
python3 analysis/build_insights.py --only tool-projector,tool-pace-band,tool-weather-match,tool-course-goal --output public/data/insights
node scripts/verify-insights.cjs
```

Rebuilding with `--only` keeps every other family's file and rewrites the manifest. The manifest binds every `insights_*.py` module's SHA-256, so editing a tool builder without rebuilding fails the verifier.

## Verification

- `scripts/verify-insights.cjs` independently recounts each tool family from the release shards. It checks:
  - the tool cohort;
  - every published projector cell's finishes and editions, and that no 100+ group is missing;
  - exact percentiles, slowdown splits and remaining pace for every All-courses cell at 20 and 30 km;
  - every pace-band group count, with sampled medians and slowdown shares;
  - the weather editions, rows, matched editions and edition-balanced shares;
  - the course-goal rows, the unavailable list and the course list.
- `scripts/verify-tools.cjs` (in `npm run verify:data`) checks golden values (225 on October 8, 2026): parsing, split tables, the pace charts' even-pace values recomputed by hand, the goal-page list, slugs and links, Riegel and Daniels values, Tanda's range, heat formulas against published tables, age rules and standards for every race, the Boston downhill index and cut-off history, and split reading; since October 15, 2026 (306 checks) also the result handoffs: each link's form and range against what the pace band and `readAnalysisProfile` read, and the rendered cards; and since the race planner (371 checks, about 16 s, most of it reading the runner records) the planner's link, goal reading and course-goal rows, with one row recounted from the records ([above](#race-planner)).
- `analysis/test_insights.py` (`Tools` class) covers:
  - the slug rule;
  - projector bands, trend variants and the slowdown split;
  - the pace-band window boundary (3:55:00 is not in the 3:55 window);
  - the weather three-edition gate and edition balancing;
  - the course-goal tolerance and gates.

## Privacy

Tools compute in the browser and send no typed values to any server (the course and recorded-gender choices only select which public data file is fetched from the site's host); session replays mask what is typed and leave out `.tool-results` panels and the gender or division choice (see [ANALYTICS.md](ANALYTICS.md)). A new tool must render its inputs inside `.tool-workspace` and its results inside `.tool-results`, which `scripts/verify-analytics.cjs` checks. Tool state is kept in the URL so a visitor can share a link: goals, paces, splits, course and units. Typing updates the URL with `history.replaceState`, which makes no request. The site's referrer policy is `strict-origin` (root `metadata.referrer`), and `loadInsight` fetches data with `referrerPolicy: 'no-referrer'`, so later requests never carry the query string. Opening a shared link does send that URL to the host, like any page request. A birth date is never in the URL. The qualifying checker keeps it in component state, plus `localStorage` only if the visitor ticks "remember on this device". Projector runner cards and the start-line clock are stored only in `localStorage`; the start clock expires after 12 hours, and card labels never go into links. Analytics records only the tool's path (`/tools/<slug>`); `lib/analytics-policy.ts` and `lib/replay-policy.ts` strip every query string and fragment from events and recordings, and `scripts/verify-analytics.cjs` tests that tool inputs are removed.

## Known limitations

- **Referrer on first load:** the referrer policy is a `<meta>` tag, so on its own it would let the CSS and JavaScript requested before it is parsed send the page URL to the same host. On Vercel, `vercel.json` also sends an HTTP `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin` header on every path, which closes that gap. A build served without that header (the optional GitHub Pages build) still sends the URL on those first requests, to the host that already received it with the page request.
- **Projector shard size:** the All-courses projector shards are about 80–95 KB gzipped, because they include the men and women variants. Splitting those into separate files would make each smaller.
- **Course chooser temperatures:** a course's start-temperature range covers all of its editions, not only the editions in the row, and the page labels it that way.
- **Projector at 40 km:** the projector is no more accurate than even pace at 40 km (see the accuracy table), and says so on the page.
- **Boston window end:** the end of Boston 2028's qualifying window (2027 registration week) is not yet dated. The checker treats times after September 30, 2027 as outside it and flags September 2027.

## Annual maintenance: qualifying standards

Qualifying standards and windows change every year. `lib/tools/qualifying.ts` records `VERIFIED_AT` (currently 2026-10-08), and every checker card and race page shows it. The review covers the checker and the race pages (`/tools/qualifying/<race>`) together: both read the same `STANDARDS`, and the race pages' sitemap `lastmod` is `VERIFIED_AT`. Before each registration season, and at least every September:

1. **Boston.** Check the standards for the next edition, the qualifying window start, the age date (race day), and the downhill-course rule (the index thresholds in `bostonDownhillIndex`). After registration closes, append the new cut-off, field and not-accepted counts to `BOSTON_CUTOFFS` from the B.A.A. announcement.
2. **New York.** Check the time-qualifier bands, which NYRR races count, the age rule and the application window.
3. **London Good For Age.** Check the bands, the window, the strictly-under rule, the UK-residency rule and the application dates.
4. **Chicago.** Check the bands, including the youngest band's range, the window and the application dates.
5. **Berlin.** Check the birth-year bands and registration dates.
6. **Sydney.** Check the High Performance Program standards and window.
7. Update `sources`, then `VERIFIED_AT`. Update the golden values in `scripts/verify-tools.cjs` and run `npm run verify:data`.
8. Never forecast a cut-off. The cut-off table shows only past cut-offs the visitor's margin would have cleared.
9. **Race pages.** Open each `/tools/qualifying/<race>` page in a build. Check the title and heading year (read from `edition`), the at-a-glance card, the window end (an application deadline or an undated end is shown as such), the entry text and the per-race words in `components/tools/QualifyingRaceText.tsx` (organiser, entry route, page noun, search name). A race added to `STANDARDS` gets a page, a sitemap entry and a share card from that list, but also needs those word maps and its course in `QUALIFYING_COURSE` (`lib/tools/qualifying.ts`). If a race name changes, run `npm run og:images` and commit the new card. Then `npm run build` and `npm run verify:seo`.

Dated application windows show an "open now / upcoming / closed" badge. Where a race states a time of day (London 16:00 GMT; Chicago 8 a.m. and 2 p.m. CT), `opensAt` and `closesAt` hold the UTC instant and the badge compares it with the current time. Otherwise it uses the visitor's calendar date. A stale entry looks closed rather than wrong, but it still needs this review.

Points checked against official pages on 2026-10-07:
- **Boston:** the downhill index uses the B.A.A.'s own metric bounds for drops typed in metres: 457.2, 914.2 and 1,828.6 m. These are not exact conversions of 1,500, 3,000 and 6,000 ft. The 2028 race date (April 17) is confirmed. The end of the qualifying window, 2027 registration week, is not yet dated.
- **Time equal to the standard:** New York ("at least as fast as") and Chicago ("within the standards") state that an equal time qualifies. Boston, Berlin and Sydney do not, and the checker says it assumes an equal time counts. London requires strictly under.
- **New York:** NYRR guaranteed entry needs a time from the 2026 TCS New York City Marathon (November 1, 2026) or a listed NYRR half. The pool cut-offs (2025: 13:20, top 25%; 2026: 22:52, top 10%) are both from NYRR press releases.

## Adding a tool

1. Add an entry to `lib/tools/registry.ts` with its evidence kinds and, for data tools, its index file.
2. Create `app/tools/<slug>/page.tsx` (server: metadata, `ToolHeader`, the client component, `ToolNext`) and `components/tools/<Component>.tsx`. Put the tool's method, limits and sources on `/methodology` (`components/methods/MethodsTools.tsx`, a `Detail` with id `tools-<slug>`), never on the tool page; badges go through `EvidenceTag`.
3. Label every panel with `EvidencePanel`. Follow the repository contracts in `AGENTS.md`: say "sustained slowdown" with the citation; observed shares, never "your chance"; no data-based weather or elevation adjustment; no invented splits; groups of at least 100 finishes.
4. For a new data family, add an `insights_tool_<name>.py` module, register it in `optional_families()` with a `tool-` prefix, add a recount to `scripts/verify-insights.cjs` and the module to `toolModules`, and add tests.
5. Put every form field inside `.tool-workspace` and every result inside `.tool-results`, so session replays mask the inputs and leave the results out. Wrap any goal-dependent text outside those (headings, aria-labels, gender or division choices) in `ph-no-capture`.
6. Add the path to analytics automatically through the registry, and run `npm run verify:data`, `npm run verify:analytics`, `npm run build` and the Python tests.
