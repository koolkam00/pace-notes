# Pace Notes: agent entry point

Read [docs/PROJECT_HANDOFF.md](docs/PROJECT_HANDOFF.md) first, then the relevant data, website, analysis, operations, and issue documents linked there. This file adds navigation and restates current project contracts; it does not replace the detailed instructions in [analysis/README.md](analysis/README.md) or release documentation.

## Working agreements

- Make the source code, complete runner records (including recorded names), exports, database snapshots, overlays and analysis outputs publicly downloadable without an account. Use ordinary unencrypted files; compression is optional. This current access policy supersedes earlier privacy-only instructions.
- Keep passwords, access tokens and other operational credentials out of datasets and repository files. They are not research data. Public dataset downloads must not depend on credentials or expiring signed URLs.
- Half-marathon figures use the separate pin `analysis/half-release.json` and the reviewed policy file it names; linked figures also bind the adopted marathon release, its runner manifest and the weather evidence. The half results, the links between half and marathon records and the scripts that built them are the owner's public CC BY 4.0 releases (`half-dataset-20261029-1202`, doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.34858428.v1) under the public-download policy above (owner decision, October 30, 2026: "publish everything and full merge"); `analysis/download_half.py` fetches them anonymously from github.com release URLs. Pages still publish aggregates: every shown group holds at least 100 finishes or pairs and any claim across editions rests on at least 3 editions, rules for estimate reliability, not for access. A verified edition is stamped in both the snapshot and the corrected edition metadata (`half-dataset-20261029-2157`, pinned by sha256 in `metadata_correction`); a new snapshot or metadata file shows no half figure until the policy is reviewed again. The pin's `private_tag` names (`half-snapshot-20261029-1133`, `runner-links-20261029-1156`) are identities the policy, the links extract and the reviewed counts are keyed by, not access labels. Half-only figures use verified editions; any figure that uses unverified editions carries the 'Includes unverified half results' chip. Linked figures use medium or high links with ns_keys ≤ 3, set B pairing, and say 'records linked by name'. A page's guard decides whether a sentence is stated: when a number moves and a guard fails, the fallback shows and the guard is re-judged on its merits, never loosened to keep a headline; a share card whose takeaway fails is withdrawn. Sustained slowdown stays a marathon metric. After any half rebuild, run the local source recount; CI fails on a stale receipt, and the workflow's `source` job rebuilds from the public assets whenever the pin, policy, builders or recount change. Read [docs/HALF_MARATHON.md](docs/HALF_MARATHON.md).
- Every visible analysis must use the adopted release in analysis/release.json (including supporting study and runner lookup); weather must match through its separate pin. Do not fall back to historical chart files. Preserve historical numerical evidence in Git history and immutable releases. live.json is current compatibility metadata, with no archived figure payloads. Importers have separate ownership and validation contracts.
- Runner context must additionally match the exact runner-manifest hash, timestamp and edition mapping. Refresh/import the lookup before rebuilding context, then use its dedicated importer and full verifier. Same-edition peer groups require 101 finishes; finish percentiles exclude the focused record, while pace quartiles include it. Weather and supplied-route elevation remain context, never personal-exposure measurements or finish-time adjustments. One exception, by owner decision on October 23, 2026 ("It should use an elevation model"): the planner's and pace band's terrain splits divide the visitor's own goal over a corrected, audited route profile (`analysis/course_terrain/corrections.json`) with one published grade curve. They never change the goal or any finish time, are never fitted to Pace Notes data, never use an uncorrected supplied profile, carry the Published research badge, and ship only on courses marked `band: "terrain"`. Read [docs/RUNNER_CONTEXT_AND_PEERS.md](docs/RUNNER_CONTEXT_AND_PEERS.md) for source conflicts and interpretation.
- All-finisher course/weather/downhill views bind both exact runner and context manifests; rebuild after either changes. Their cross-edition rates average editions equally, while onset and finish-time context pool finishes. Keep those denominators distinct and retain history for personal improvement and other longitudinal questions. Read [docs/ALL_FINISHER_ANALYSES.md](docs/ALL_FINISHER_ANALYSES.md).
- Runner tools (`/tools`) label Pace Notes data, published research and official standards with badges and never combine them. Plain calculations from the visitor's own inputs carry no badge. The owner does not want the word "arithmetic" anywhere on the site; `verify-tools` fails if it appears in `app/`, `components/` or `lib/`. Data shares are observed shares of complete finishes, never "your chance". Qualifying standards are dated and need the annual review in [docs/TOOLS.md](docs/TOOLS.md). Birth dates never go in URLs or analytics. The as-run course shape (medians of finishes that made a goal, scaled to it) is retired: observed medians near a goal appear only unscaled, in a Pace Notes data panel, with the sustained-slowdown forewarning, never as a band or on a wristband.
- Use Pace Notes branding and sustained slowdown terminology in public prose. Preserve the metric: slowdown at least 25% for at least 5 km after 20 km relative to the 5–20 km baseline. Its definition and neutral published-method citation are stated once, on /methodology#sustained-slowdown; other pages link the term there instead of repeating them. Historical field names and compatibility URLs may remain unchanged.
- Pages show insights only (owner decision, October 16, 2026). Methods, definitions, data sources, coverage and limits live only on /methodology (Methods & sources): no calculation, source, coverage, cohort or method-caveat text on any other page. Evidence badges stay as short labels that link to /methodology#evidence, and the sustained-slowdown definition and its published-method citation live on /methodology#sustained-slowdown, where pages link the term. Functional copy (labels, how to use a tool, empty and error states) and short wording disclaimers such as "Not a prediction" stay. Every /methodology#<id> link must name an id the page has (`verify-seo` `methods-anchors`). Orientation (owner request, October 22, 2026): the home hero says in one line what Pace Notes is; /about says what it is, where to start (a row per visitor goal), why it exists and how it is made, and links to the /methodology ids for where the data comes from, how releases are checked, the limits and the downloads instead of repeating them; /methodology opens on a four-fact short version.
- Distinguish release-note claims, measured audit results, repository state and deployed state. Never assume a newer upload has reached the website.
- Respect per-release ID semantics. The audited `private-export-20260911-1107`, `private-export-20260912-0934`, `htw-db-2026-10-16-1652` and `private-export-20261021-1050` use canonical raw/feature record IDs with runtime uniqueness, label and full-timing checks. IDs are not stable across releases: 1652 replaced 116,287 0934 IDs (Chicago 2018, Chicago 2019 and Frankfurt 2019 were reloaded), so never carry an ID from one release to another without the runner-manifest mapping (1050 kept every 1652 ID it still holds and changed none of their records). The reviewed `private-export-20261021-0829` is not adopted: its FULL lacks the cross-race identity. September 7 CORE/FULL IDs remain incompatible and require the legacy one-to-one natural match. Never infer record alignment from row order or treat cross-race IDs as independently verified people.
- Apply the reviewed release-specific edition policy in `analysis/source_quality.py` after timing checks. Keep its exclusion counts and hashes with every result. Invalid, incomplete, held and selected-field editions cannot supply outcomes or earlier benchmarks. Missing age or recorded gender alone does not remove usable timings from the All cohort.
- Do not infer missing splits, exact ages, actual halfway timings, historical course validity, runner goals or causal effects.
- Use the existing analysis and import commands in [docs/OPERATIONS.md](docs/OPERATIONS.md). For calculation changes run Python tests; for site/data changes run npm run verify:data and npm run build. Documentation-only changes require checking links, mappings and factual consistency, not a new full-data calculation.
- Update the relevant handoff documents when changing data contracts, analysis methods, workflow behavior or issue status. Date evidence and preserve historical vintage labels.
- Prefer public GitHub Release assets for large Parquet/SQLite files and backups, with durable anonymous download links, schemas and SHA-256 checksums. Runner data may be opened or stored in the checkout; there is no mandatory privacy boundary around it. Keep statistical cohort thresholds for estimate reliability, not as a restriction on access to full records.
- Preserve legacy release tags and filenames containing `private` as compatibility identifiers; their names do not determine current access. Check the dated access-verification status in docs/PROJECT_HANDOFF.md before claiming anonymous access works.

## Practices that have paid off

Short lessons from earlier work; the linked documents hold the detail.

- **State which of four things a claim is:** a release note, a measured result (with its date and build), repository state or deployed state. Read the live site before saying anything is live.
- **Prove anonymous access, and date it.** Use a ranged GET on the github.com release URL with no credentials (`curl -sSL -r 0-0 -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' URL`, expect 206). In these sessions api.github.com requests carry injected credentials, so they prove nothing, and the asset host answers HEAD with 401. doi.org's 302 shows a DOI resolves; figshare's pages answer scripted clients with a 202 challenge.
- **Pin every input by sha256, corrections included,** and fail loudly when a frozen list, a count or a digest differs. Update a reviewed count from a real run after reading the difference, never with `--accept-counts` alone.
- **Floors and guards are different tools.** Builder floors (100 per group, 3 editions, 10 per edge slice) decide what is published; when a tail or bin falls under one, narrow the published range rather than lower the floor. Page guards decide what is said; let a failing guard fall back.
- **Numbers on pages come from data files, never from typed copy;** a verifier recounts each one. Documents quote numbers with the date and build they came from.
- **Look at what you ship.** Regenerate share cards and open the PNGs; capture changed pages at 390 and 1440 px in light and dark and read them. Text extracted from rendered HTML can show a space between React text nodes that the page does not have.
- **One heavy job at a time** on a small machine (builds, verifiers, browser scenarios, captures, source recounts); run Python that reads downloaded data with `python3 -I`, keep downloads in their own folder, and delete large temporary files.
- **Never weaken a check to make it pass.** Find the cause; if a number legitimately moved, change the expectation with a dated comment saying why.
